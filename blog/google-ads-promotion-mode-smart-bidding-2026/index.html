Google Ads Promotion Mode & the Budget-Limited Bidding Change — Explained

Google Ads rolled out two separate but related updates to how its automated bidding behaves: a new beta feature called Promotion Mode for running temporary sale-period campaigns, and a quieter backend change to how bid targets get optimized for campaigns that are limited by budget — the second one starting August 17, 2026, according to Google Ads Liaison Ginny Marvin as reported by Search Engine Journal. Neither update requires a new campaign type to understand, but both change how your existing Smart Bidding campaigns behave, which is reason enough to know what's actually different.

Google Ads has introduced Promotion Mode, a beta tool for running temporary promotional campaigns, alongside a separate change to how backend bidding optimizes budget-limited campaigns. Google's own Accelerate blog, in a post titled "Bidding and budgeting updates to scale your growth," announced both changes together, and trade coverage from Search Engine Land, Search Engine Journal, and PPC Land through mid-to-late 2026 filled in the rollout timeline and mechanics in more detail. They're easy to conflate because Google announced them together, but they work differently and affect different advertisers — worth untangling before deciding whether either one matters for your account.

Promotion Mode: a temporary lever for Search and Performance Max

Promotion Mode is built for a specific, recurring advertiser problem: a short-term sale or promotional window where an advertiser wants more volume than their standing Target ROAS or Target CPA bid strategy would normally deliver, without having to manually adjust (and later remember to revert) their core bidding strategy. Per trade coverage, Promotion Mode lets an advertiser temporarily loosen their Target ROAS tolerance and optionally add extra daily budget for a defined date range, available in beta for Search and Performance Max campaigns. One industry report traced the feature's rollout back to an initial test in September 2025 focused on campaigns using total budgets, which Google later broadened to campaigns using standard daily budgets as well.

The practical value is in the "temporary" part. Google Ads has long had seasonality adjustments as a tool for telling Smart Bidding to expect a short-term change in conversion rate around a known event — but seasonality adjustments don't add budget or change ROAS tolerance on their own; they just recalibrate what the bidding algorithm expects to see. Promotion Mode is a more direct lever specifically for the "I want more volume during this window, even at a slightly different efficiency target, and I want it to snap back automatically afterward" scenario — Black Friday, a quarter-end clearance, a grand-opening push, or any bounded sale period a business runs on a calendar.

The August 17 change: bid targets for budget-limited campaigns

The second update is less visible but arguably more universally relevant, because it changes default behavior rather than adding an opt-in feature. Per Search Engine Journal's reporting on a statement from Google Ads Liaison Ginny Marvin, Google adjusted how backend bid-target optimization works specifically for campaigns that are limited by budget — meaning campaigns where the daily budget itself, not the bid strategy, is the main constraint on how much the campaign spends and delivers. PPC Land's coverage reports the rollout began August 17, 2026 and took a few weeks to reach all affected accounts, with advertisers notified through account-level notifications inside Google Ads rather than needing to request or opt into the change.

Marvin's stated goal, as quoted in that coverage, is more predictable performance against CPA and ROAS targets for budget-limited campaigns — particularly in the moment when an advertiser raises that campaign's budget. That's a real, specific pain point: a campaign that's been budget-constrained for weeks, then gets a budget increase, has historically sometimes seen a rocky adjustment period as Smart Bidding recalibrates to the new ceiling, occasionally overspending or under-delivering against target efficiency during that transition. This change is Google's attempt to smooth that specific transition.

This is the detail worth sitting with: unlike Promotion Mode, which an advertiser actively chooses to turn on, the budget-limited bid-target change applies automatically to qualifying campaigns. If you've ever raised a Google Ads budget and watched performance wobble for a few days before settling, this is Google explicitly naming that exact friction point as something it changed the backend handling for — not a new feature you need to adopt, just a shift in behavior you may already be benefiting from without having done anything.

Why both updates point the same direction

Taken together, these two changes describe a consistent theme in how Google Ads has been evolving its automated bidding through 2026: giving advertisers more deliberate control over the moments when they want bidding to behave differently from its steady-state optimization — a temporary sale window, a budget increase — rather than only offering a single, static bid strategy that's supposed to handle every situation identically. That's broadly consistent with other 2026 Smart Bidding coverage, including the expansion of Smart Bidding Exploration (a feature that lets the algorithm test slightly outside its usual targeting boundaries to find incremental conversions) to all feed-less Performance Max campaigns and, in beta, to feed-based Performance Max and Shopping campaigns.

For a small business running Google Ads without a dedicated in-house PPC team, the honest takeaway is that Google's automated bidding keeps getting more capable at handling specific real-world scenarios — but only if someone is actually watching the account closely enough to know which scenario applies, and when a feature like Promotion Mode is worth switching on versus when the default automatic improvements are already doing the job.

What to actually check in your own account

If you manage Google Ads for your own business or a client, here's where this actually translates into something to do:

Check Google Ads account notifications for anything mentioning bid-target or budget-limited changes. Since the August 17 update applies automatically and Google notifies advertisers through in-product notifications, it's worth confirming whether your account received one and reviewing performance on any budget-limited campaigns around that window, rather than assuming nothing changed.

If you have a recurring sale or promotional calendar, test Promotion Mode on one campaign before relying on it broadly. A beta feature is exactly that — worth testing on a single Search or Performance Max campaign during a real but non-critical promotional window first, comparing results against how that same campaign performed during a past promotion without it, before making it a standing part of every sale you run.

Revisit any campaign you've recently given a significant budget increase. If a campaign has been budget-limited for a while and you're planning to raise its budget, this is a genuinely better moment to do it than it might have been a year ago — but it's still worth watching performance closely in the days immediately after, rather than assuming the backend change means zero monitoring is needed.

Don't conflate "new feature" with "requires action." Promotion Mode needs you to opt in per campaign. The budget-limited bid-target change doesn't need you to do anything except understand that it's happening — the only action item there is checking whether it's actually improved your specific accounts' behavior around budget changes, using your own before-and-after performance data rather than taking Google's framing on faith.

Frequently asked questions

What is Google Ads Promotion Mode?

Promotion Mode is a beta feature for Search and Performance Max campaigns that lets advertisers temporarily loosen their Target ROAS and add an optional extra daily budget for a defined promotional window, such as a sale period, without permanently changing their standing bidding strategy.

What changed for budget-limited Google Ads campaigns on August 17, 2026?

Per Search Engine Journal's reporting on Google Ads Liaison Ginny Marvin's statement, Google began adjusting backend bid-target optimization for campaigns that are limited by budget, aiming for more predictable delivery against CPA and ROAS targets, especially once a budget increases. The rollout began August 17, 2026 and took a few weeks to reach all affected accounts, with advertisers notified through account-level notifications rather than needing to opt in.

Do I need to change anything in my Google Ads account for this?

For the budget-limited bid-target change, no action is required since it affects backend optimization automatically. Promotion Mode, by contrast, is something an advertiser actively turns on for a specific campaign and date range when they want it, and is not applied unless chosen.
