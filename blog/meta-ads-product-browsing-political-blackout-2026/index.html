Meta Turns On Product Browsing for Every Live Ad Oct 28 — And Blocks New Political Ads Before the Midterms

Two unrelated Meta advertiser notices landed in the same week in October 2026. One quietly changes how every existing Facebook and Instagram ad behaves unless you opt out. The other blocks an entire category of new ads for eight days around the US midterms. Here's exactly what each one does, who it affects, and what's still genuinely unconfirmed.

K
Kryvex Media Team
Nov 1, 2026 · 10 min read

Two separate Meta advertiser notices surfaced within days of each other in October 2026, and they're easy to confuse if you only catch the headlines. The first changes a default setting inside ads that are already running — Meta plans to switch on "product browsing" for existing Facebook and Instagram ads starting October 28, 2026, unless an advertiser opts out first. The second is unrelated to product ads at all: a week-long block on new US political, electoral, and social-issue ads ahead of the midterms, running from October 27 through November 3. Trade outlets including PPC Land and RelevantAudience reported both based on Meta's own advertiser notices, though neither change appears to have a full, dated post on Meta's public Business Help Center or developer blog that we could independently confirm every detail against — so this piece is explicit about what's solidly sourced and what's still secondary-reporting-only.

Change one: product browsing becomes the default inside existing ads

Product browsing lets someone scroll through a small catalog of products without leaving the ad unit — instead of a single static image or video that clicks straight through to your site, the person taps through multiple products inside the ad itself before deciding whether to continue to your landing page. According to the reporting on Meta's notice, this format is not new; what's changing is the default. Meta is reportedly switching product browsing on for ads that are already live — not just new ads created after the change — starting October 28, 2026. If you don't take action, ads you built months ago under the old behavior will start showing this new in-ad experience automatically.

Per the notice as reported, Meta's own justification is a claimed average 3% lift in conversions from in-app product browsing. That figure comes from Meta, not an independent study — treat it as a vendor claim, not a verified benchmark for your account.

How to opt out, if you want to

The opt-out, per the coverage, lives inside Advantage+ Creative and Destination settings and can reportedly be toggled per individual ad rather than only account-wide — meaning you don't necessarily have to choose between "all ads get product browsing" or "none do." A few things are worth being upfront about, because the secondary reporting doesn't settle them cleanly:

  • Whether a product catalog is strictly required for the experience to appear isn't addressed consistently across the coverage we found.
  • Whether the opt-out applies per ad account or across an entire business portfolio also isn't settled in the reporting — if you manage multiple ad accounts, check each one rather than assuming a single toggle covers everything.
  • One trade write-up noted advertiser chatter that this setting has quietly existed inside Advantage+ Creative settings for roughly two months before the "existing ads" default-on change was announced — which, if accurate, means some accounts may already have exposure to this format in a way they didn't notice.

The practical move, regardless of those open questions: log into Ads Manager, check Advantage+ Creative settings on your active campaigns before October 28, and make a deliberate choice rather than letting the default apply by inaction. If your product presentation, pricing structure, or brand guidelines depend on a controlled, single-image ad experience, this is worth five minutes of checking per account.

Who this actually affects

This matters most for advertisers running Advantage+ catalog or shopping ads where a product catalog already exists — the feature has somewhere to pull from. It matters less, or possibly not at all, for lead-generation or brand-awareness campaigns with no catalog attached, though the exact mechanics of what happens to a non-catalog ad under this change aren't fully confirmed in the reporting available. Any business running Meta ads toward an online store or product catalog should treat this as an active decision to make, not a passive one.

Change two: the pre-midterm political ad blackout

Separately, and unrelated to the product browsing change, Meta published a notice on its developer blog on October 6, 2026 — per PPC Land's reporting — establishing a restriction period on new US political, electoral, and social-issue ads. The window: 12:01 AM Pacific on October 27 through 11:59 PM Pacific on November 3, 2026. (One other outlet's write-up gave November 4 as the end date rather than November 3; the November 3 figure is the one that matches the quoted notice text in PPC Land's reporting, so we're flagging the discrepancy rather than picking silently.)

  • What's blocked: new ads about social issues, elections, or politics created or launched during the window.
  • What's allowed: ads of that category that were already running and had served at least one impression before the window opened can continue to run, and can reportedly be paused and unpaused during the blackout — though the reporting doesn't clarify whether a paused ad can be reactivated mid-window with full confidence.
  • Edits: existing political/social-issue ads face limited ability to be edited during the restriction period, consistent with how Meta has run this kind of blackout around past US elections.
  • Comparison to 2024: Meta's 2024 pre-election restriction window ran from October 29 through November 5 — eight days. The 2026 window, per this reporting, runs seven days (October 27 to November 3), making it slightly shorter and starting slightly earlier relative to election day.

Meta had signaled this was coming back in February 2026, saying publicly it would "continue to block new political ads on our platforms during the final week of the campaign" ahead of the midterms — so the blackout itself isn't a surprise, just the specific dates confirmed closer to the vote.

Does this affect a typical small business advertiser?

For the overwhelming majority of Kryvex's clients — local service businesses, e-commerce stores, restaurants, healthcare practices — the political ad blackout is irrelevant unless your ad explicitly touches a social issue, election, or political topic as Meta classifies it. Meta's ad categorization for this purpose has occasionally caught non-political advertisers off guard in past election cycles (an ad referencing a ballot measure tangential to a local business's actual message, for instance), so if your copy touches anything that could plausibly read as issue-based, it's worth a quick self-check against Meta's advertising policies before October 27 rather than finding out mid-blackout that a routine ad got swept into the restricted category.

The product browsing default-on change is the one that actually touches most advertisers running catalog or shopping ads, regardless of industry. It's a genuine UX change to ads you may not have revisited in months, landing on a specific date with an opt-out window that closes once the default flips.

A short, practical checklist before October 28

  1. Open Ads Manager and review Advantage+ Creative and Destination settings on every active ad account, not just your primary one.
  2. Decide deliberately whether product browsing fits your brand and catalog — don't let the default apply by default.
  3. If you run any ad that references a social issue, election, or political topic, review it against Meta's ad policies before October 27 and make sure it's already live with at least one impression if you want it to be able to continue running through the blackout.
  4. Document whichever settings you land on, so a teammate (or an agency) checking the account later understands it was a deliberate choice, not an oversight.
  5. Re-check performance a week or two after October 28 if you leave product browsing on — a format change to live ads is exactly the kind of thing worth measuring rather than assuming the claimed 3% lift applies to your specific account and audience.

The bigger pattern: Meta keeps shifting defaults on live ads

This isn't the first time Meta has changed behavior on ads that are already running rather than only on newly created ones — Advantage+ placements, automated creative enhancements, and various AI-driven optimizations have followed a similar pattern over the past two years: ship the new behavior as an opt-out default rather than an opt-in choice. The practical lesson for any business running Meta ads is the same one that applies across most automated ad platforms now, including the kind of default shifts we've covered inside Google's Performance Max changes: "set and forget" campaign management increasingly means missing changes that alter how your ads actually look and perform. A quarterly settings review — not just a performance review — is a reasonable standing habit for any account spending meaningfully on Meta.

Frequently asked questions

What is Meta's new product browsing feature for existing ads?

Product browsing lets someone scroll through a catalog of products directly inside an ad unit on Facebook or Instagram, instead of clicking straight through to a landing page. According to trade press coverage of Meta's advertiser notice, Meta plans to switch this on by default for ads that are already running, starting October 28, 2026, with an opt-out available in Advantage+ Creative and Destination settings.

Can advertisers opt out of product browsing being added to their existing ads?

Yes, based on the reporting around Meta's notice, the opt-out sits in Advantage+ Creative and Destination settings and can reportedly be toggled per ad, not just account-wide. Advertisers who want to keep their existing ad experience unchanged need to actively turn it off rather than assume the old behavior stays the default.

When does Meta's 2026 political ad blackout start and end?

Per coverage of Meta's October 6, 2026 notice on the Meta for Developers blog, the restriction period runs from 12:01 AM Pacific on October 27 through 11:59 PM Pacific on November 3, 2026, blocking new US ads about social issues, elections, or politics. This is shorter than Meta's 2024 window, which ran from October 29 through November 5.
