Google Merged Its Shopping Ads and Free Listings Policies in September 2026 — Here's What Changed

Google announced on July 15, 2026 that it would fold its two previously separate rulebooks — Shopping ads policies and free listing policies — into a single set of Shopping policies, effective that September. Google's own language says the merge shouldn't add new restrictions on its own. Here's what the change log actually says, what else moved around it, and what's worth checking in a Merchant Center account now that it's in effect.

K
Kryvex Media Team
Nov 2, 2026 · 9 min read

For years, a merchant running Google Shopping had to read two separate rulebooks: one set of policies for paid Shopping ads, and a different set for free product listings. On July 15, 2026, Google announced it was retiring that split. According to Google's own Merchant Center policy update, the Shopping ads policies and the free listing policies were consolidated into a single set of Shopping policies inside the Merchant Center Help Center starting in September 2026, described by Google as a change made "for improved organization and clarity." Google was explicit about scope in its own announcement language: merchants should not expect new listing restrictions as a direct result of the consolidation itself.

What actually merged, and what stayed separate inside it

The practical effect is structural rather than substantive on its own — one combined policy document replaces two, rather than Google inventing new rules wholesale. Inside that merged document, rules that apply only to paid ads are specifically labeled as such, so a merchant running only free listings (not paid Shopping campaigns) can tell at a glance which sections apply and which don't. If you use only free listings, the ad-only rules don't apply to you, but the shared Shopping policies — covering product data accuracy, prohibited content, and the general eligibility requirements both formats have always had in common — apply either way.

For a small business that has only ever run free listings, or only ever run paid Shopping campaigns, the honest takeaway is: check the merged document once to confirm nothing specific to your setup changed, but don't expect to find a long list of brand-new restrictions. For a business running both formats side by side, the merge is arguably a net improvement — one reference document instead of two, with overlapping requirements no longer duplicated in two places that could theoretically drift out of sync with each other over time.

"No new restrictions as a direct result of this update" is Google's own framing, not an independent guarantee. It describes the policy-merge mechanism itself, not every other Merchant Center change happening around the same time — several of which, covered below, do add real new requirements on their own separate timelines.

A quick refresher on why there were two policy sets in the first place

Free product listings on Google Shopping are a relatively recent addition to a much older paid system. Google's shopping product search dates back to Froogle in 2002, became a fully paid listing system in 2012, and then Google reversed that in April 2020 — its then-president of commerce, Bill Ready, announced that starting that April, most results in the Shopping tab would consist of free product listings rather than paid ones, a response to the retail disruption of the pandemic. Free listings first appeared only on the Shopping tab itself before expanding, by August 2020, into relevant placements on core Google Search and Google Images results too, and later into additional countries beyond the US. Because free listings and paid Shopping ads were added to Merchant Center at different points for different reasons, their policies were written and maintained separately for years — which is exactly the structural overlap-and-drift problem the September 2026 merge is meant to clean up.

One additional wrinkle worth knowing, reported by an independent researcher in April 2026: sponsored tiles have begun appearing inside what had been a purely organic free-listing grid on the Shopping tab since 2020. That's a separate observation from the official policy-merge announcement — it describes ad placement inside the free-listing surface, not a change to the policies governing eligibility — but it's a useful reminder that "free listings" and "paid ads" are becoming less cleanly separated as actual placements on the page, even as Google frames the underlying policy documentation as being reorganized for clarity rather than convergence.

Other real changes on Google's 2026 Merchant Center change log

The policy consolidation sits alongside several other, separately dated changes that Google's own Merchant Center announcements log documents for 2026. Worth knowing about, since they're easy to miss if a merchant only read about the policy merge itself:

  • A new pickup-cost attribute for local inventory ads and free local listings, part of Google's annual product data specification update — relevant to any merchant offering in-store pickup who wants that cost reflected accurately in local listings.
  • A political content restriction for Shopping ads, effective April 16, 2026 — relevant mainly to merchants in or adjacent to that category, but worth a quick check if a product catalog could plausibly be read as touching political content even indirectly.
  • Performance reporting changes that began August 24, 2026 — affecting how certain performance metrics are presented inside Merchant Center and Google Ads reporting for Shopping campaigns, separate from the policy document itself.

None of these three is the policy consolidation — they're genuinely separate changes that happened to land in the same general window. A merchant checking "did anything change with Google Shopping this year" should look at all of them, not assume the single policy-merge announcement covers everything that moved.

Why this is worth a merchant's actual attention, not just a skim

Shopping feed and policy compliance issues are one of the more common, avoidable sources of wasted ad spend and suppressed free-listing visibility for small ecommerce businesses — a disapproved product, a misclassified category, or an incomplete required attribute can silently reduce how often a product shows at all, with no obvious error message pointing a merchant back to the exact cause. A policy reorganization is a reasonable prompt to actually re-read the current rules in full, rather than relying on institutional memory of how the old, separately documented rules used to work. That's true even when — as Google states here — the reorganization itself isn't meant to tighten anything.

This is especially true for a small business that set up its Merchant Center account once, years ago, and hasn't revisited the policy pages since. Product data requirements, prohibited-content categories, and the specific attributes Google expects (brand, GTIN, availability, shipping details, and now the pickup-cost attribute noted above) have all been refined multiple times since most small merchants first configured their feed. A policy document reorganization is a low-effort, genuinely useful trigger to re-verify the basics — not because the reorganization itself demands it, but because it's a natural calendar reminder that the underlying rules have likely moved since the feed was last reviewed end to end.

A practical checklist for Merchant Center accounts right now

  1. Read the current merged Shopping policies document in full rather than relying on a summary of what used to be two separate documents — note which sections are marked ad-only versus shared.
  2. Confirm the pickup-cost attribute is populated if your business offers local in-store pickup through Google's local inventory ads or free local listings.
  3. Check product categorization against the political-content restriction if there's any plausible overlap, even indirectly (certain apparel, media, or advocacy-adjacent merchandise categories are the ones most likely to trigger an unexpected review).
  4. Review current performance reports with the August 2026 reporting changes in mind before drawing conclusions from a month-over-month comparison that spans the change date.
  5. Audit disapproved products and policy warnings inside Merchant Center directly — a reorganized policy document is a natural trigger to resolve any standing issues rather than let them keep silently suppressing visibility.

Frequently asked questions

What did Google actually change with its Shopping policy consolidation?

Google announced on July 15, 2026 that in September 2026 it would merge its previously separate Shopping ads policies and free listing policies into one unified set of Shopping policies inside the Merchant Center Help Center, for clarity and organization. Google stated that merchants should not expect new listing restrictions as a direct result of the merge itself.

Do the merged policies apply the same way to paid ads and free listings?

No. Within the single merged policy document, Google labels which specific rules apply only to paid Shopping ads. A merchant using only free listings is still bound by the shared Shopping policies that apply to both, but is not subject to the ad-only rules called out separately in the document.

What else changed in Google Merchant Center around the same time?

Google's 2026 Merchant Center change log also lists a new pickup-cost attribute for local inventory ads and free local listings, a restriction on political content in Shopping ads effective April 16, 2026, and performance-reporting changes that began August 24, 2026. These are separate, specific changes from the broader July policy-consolidation announcement.
