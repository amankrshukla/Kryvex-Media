Google Expanded Ads in AI Mode to 10 More Countries — You Can't Opt Out

On October 19, 2026, Google reversed a commitment it had made back in March 2026 to keep ads out of AI Mode results. Nine days later, on October 28, it followed through: ads in AI Mode expanded to 10 additional countries, and a new "multiturn" ad format went live in the United States, meaning ads can now appear not just on your first AI Mode question but in the follow-up turns of the same conversation. If you run Google Ads campaigns in any of the newly-added markets, there's a detail in this rollout worth knowing before it affects your account: you don't get asked.

K
Kryvex Media Team
Nov 10, 2026 · 10 min read

Google's AI Mode search experience now carries ads in 11 countries total, and for the vast majority of advertisers, that happened automatically — not as an opt-in choice. Per multiple outlets covering the rollout, including PPC Land and RelevantAudience, the October 28, 2026 expansion added India, Australia, Canada, Kenya, Malaysia, New Zealand, Nigeria, Pakistan, the Philippines, and Singapore to the United States, which had carried AI Mode ads since earlier in 2026. Alongside the country expansion, Google launched multiturn ads in the US specifically — ads that can now surface across a follow-up question within the same AI Mode conversation, not only the initial query.

The reversal that preceded it

This expansion didn't happen in a vacuum. According to reporting on the timeline, Google had stated in March 2026 that it intended to keep AI Mode free of ads, distinguishing it from standard Search results where ads have always appeared. On October 19, 2026, that position reportedly changed, and the October 28 expansion followed roughly a week and a half later. It's a useful reminder for any business running paid search: a platform's current stated policy on where and how ads appear is a snapshot of where things stand today, not a permanent guarantee carried forward indefinitely. Treating "Google said there'd be no ads here" as settled fact, months after the statement was made, is a real risk precisely because platforms do revisit these calls as a surface matures and proves out as a place advertisers actually want to be.

What "you can't opt out" actually means in practice

This is the detail advertisers in the newly-added countries should understand clearly. Per Google's own help documentation, as summarized across the outlets covering this rollout, existing Search, Shopping, and Performance Max campaigns become automatically eligible to serve into AI Mode once it's live in a given market — there's no separate AI Mode campaign type to opt into, and critically, no separate control to opt a given campaign out of AI Mode placements while keeping it running in standard Search. The choice isn't "Search yes, AI Mode no." It's "Search and AI Mode together, or neither."

The levers that remain available are the same ones that have always existed for standard Search campaigns: negative keywords to exclude irrelevant queries, brand exclusions, and asset pinning to control which specific headlines, descriptions, or images appear in a given placement. None of these function as an AI Mode-specific switch — they shape what shows up across all eligible surfaces, AI Mode included, rather than giving you a dedicated dial for this one surface. If AI Mode ad placements end up performing differently than standard Search placements for your account — better, worse, or just different in character — these existing tools are what you have to work with, not a way to simply exit the surface while keeping the rest of your account running as before.

Don't confuse this with a separate control that does exist: Google's publisher-side opt-out lets a website choose whether its own content gets used in AI Overviews and AI Mode responses at all. That's a decision about whether your site's content appears in AI-generated answers as an organic result. It has nothing to do with whether your paid ads can be excluded from showing inside AI Mode — those are two unrelated controls governing two different things, and reporting on this topic sometimes blurs the two together.

Reporting is folded in, not broken out

One more practical wrinkle: AI Mode ad performance currently reports inside your standard campaign and search-term reports, rather than as its own isolated line item. That means there's no native dashboard view today that answers "how is my spend doing specifically in AI Mode" on its own. If you want a rough sense of what's happening, the closest available signal is reviewing your search terms report for longer, more conversational-phrased queries — the kind of multi-clause, question-style input that's typical of how people interact with AI Mode, as opposed to the shorter, more fragment-style queries typical of a traditional search box. It's an imperfect proxy, not a real breakdown, but it's what's available until Google ships more granular reporting for this surface, if it ever does.

What "multiturn" actually changes about the ad surface

The multiturn format matters more than a simple country-count expansion, and it's worth understanding why. In standard Search, an ad is tied to one query and one results page — you search, you see an ad (or don't), that's the interaction. AI Mode is built around a back-and-forth conversation: you ask something, Google's AI responds, you ask a follow-up based on that response, and the conversation continues across several turns before you're done. Until this change, reporting indicates ad eligibility was effectively limited to the earlier parts of that exchange. Multiturn ads extend eligibility into the later turns too — meaning a conversation that starts with a broad, informational question and narrows over two or three follow-ups into something closer to purchase intent can now surface ads at that later, more qualified point in the exchange, not only at the opening question. For advertisers, that's a meaningfully different surface than a single search results page: the same user, the same session, now has more than one point where an ad placement can appear, and the query context at turn three of a conversation looks nothing like the query context at turn one.

Industry coverage of this shift has also noted a change in how Google itself talks about it. Where earlier statements framed ads in AI experiences as an ongoing experiment, more recent reporting quotes Google's advertising leadership describing these placements as no longer experimental — language that signals a shift from "we're testing this cautiously" to "this is now a standard part of how the ad platform works." For an advertiser deciding how much attention this surface deserves relative to other priorities, that framing shift is itself a useful signal: it's reasonable to expect further expansion rather than a pullback.

What this means if you advertise in one of the newly-added countries

Check whether you're actually targeting an affected market. If your Search, Shopping, or Performance Max campaigns geo-target the US or any of the 10 newly-added countries, your existing campaigns are very likely already eligible for AI Mode placements as of this rollout — there was no action required on your end for that eligibility to take effect, which also means there was no notification moment forcing you to review it, either.

Tighten negative keywords and brand exclusions now, rather than reactively. Since there's no dedicated AI Mode exclusion, the existing controls carry more relative weight than before for shaping what appears across every eligible surface, AI Mode included. If there are query types or brand contexts you wouldn't want representing you inside a conversational AI answer, now's the time to make sure those exclusions are actually in place.

Watch your search terms report for unfamiliar, conversational-style entries. Given the reporting limitation above, this is currently the most direct way to get a sense of whether AI Mode traffic is showing up distinctly in your account, even without a clean breakout.

Know this isn't the only AI-surface ad test in motion. Separately, Google has also been testing ads specifically within healthcare-related AI Mode and AI Overview results since Google Marketing Live in May 2026, limited to English-language US queries — a distinct, narrower test from this broader country expansion, but relevant context if your business operates in that vertical.

Frequently asked questions

Can I opt my campaigns out of showing in AI Mode?

Not separately from Search. Per Google's own help documentation, as reported by multiple outlets, existing Search, Shopping, and Performance Max campaigns are automatically eligible to appear in AI Mode once it's live in your country, and advertisers cannot target or opt out of AI Mode placements independently of opting out of Search advertising altogether. The available controls are negative keywords, brand exclusions, and asset pinning, not a dedicated AI Mode toggle.

Which countries have ads in AI Mode now?

As of the October 28, 2026 expansion, reporting lists ads in AI Mode as live in the United States plus 10 additional countries: India, Australia, Canada, Kenya, Malaysia, New Zealand, Nigeria, Pakistan, the Philippines, and Singapore — 11 countries in total. Some reports indicate Brazil, Mexico, and the UK are planned for later in Q4 2026, though outlets differ on the exact timing for those markets.

How do I know if my ads are already appearing in AI Mode?

Google's reporting currently folds AI Mode performance into standard campaign and search-term reports rather than breaking it out as its own line item, so there's no native way to isolate AI Mode-specific performance today. The closest practical step is reviewing your search terms report for longer, more conversational-style queries, which are more typical of how people phrase questions inside AI Mode.
