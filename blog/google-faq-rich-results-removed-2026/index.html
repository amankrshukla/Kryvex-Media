Google Ended FAQ Rich Results in 2026 — What It Means for Your Site

On May 7, 2026, Google's own documentation confirmed that the expandable question-and-answer snippet known as the FAQ rich result stopped appearing in Google Search — for every site, with no exceptions left. It's the quiet final step in a restriction Google first announced back in August 2023, and it closes a chapter a lot of small-business websites never fully understood in the first place. Here's what actually happened, what it does and doesn't mean for your FAQPage markup, and what's worth doing about it now.

Kryvex Media Team
Nov 6, 2026 · 9 min read

Google's own Search Central documentation now carries a deprecation notice: FAQ rich results stopped appearing in Google Search as of May 7, 2026. Multiple SEO trade outlets covering the change — including Search Engine Journal, TechWyse, and PPC Land — report the same date and describe it as the final step of a restriction that began nearly three years earlier. If you've never heard of a FAQ rich result, you've still probably seen one: it's the expandable list of questions and answers that used to appear directly in Google's search listings, letting a user tap a question and read the answer without ever clicking through to the page. As of this year, that feature is gone for everyone.

How we got here: the August 2023 restriction

This didn't happen overnight. Google's own Search Central blog post from August 8, 2023 ("Changes to HowTo and FAQ rich results") announced that FAQ rich results would from then on be shown only for "well-known, authoritative government and health websites" — language taken directly from Google's post. In the same announcement, Google said How-To rich results would be limited to desktop devices. For the overwhelming majority of websites — every small business, every agency client, every blog that had carefully marked up a FAQ section with FAQPage schema hoping to win that extra space in the search results — that 2023 change effectively ended FAQ rich result eligibility already. Only a narrow category of large, authoritative institutional sites kept the feature at all after that point.

That's an important piece of context missing from a lot of the 2026 coverage: for most businesses reading this, FAQ rich results had already stopped showing up for their pages more than two and a half years before this year's announcement. What changed in May 2026 is that Google finished the job — removing the feature even for the remaining government and health sites that had kept it. Search Engine Journal's reporting on the 2026 change describes it as ending eligibility for "the remaining FAQ rich results for government and health sites, too," which is the clearest framing: 2023 cut the feature down to a small group, 2026 cut it to zero.

What's being removed, and on what timeline

Coverage of the 2026 change describes a staged rollout rather than one single flip of a switch, consistent with how Google typically retires a Search feature:

  • May 7, 2026 — FAQ rich results stop appearing in Google Search results entirely. This is the date Google's own documentation update and SEJ's reporting agree on.
  • June 2026 — per multiple outlets covering the change, Google planned to remove the FAQ search-appearance documentation page, the FAQ rich result performance report inside Search Console, and FAQ support inside the Rich Results Test tool, since there's no longer a live feature for those tools to report on.
  • Later in 2026 — some coverage additionally describes FAQ rich result support being dropped from the Search Console API. Exactly which month this landed varies slightly between secondary sources, so if API-level reporting matters to your workflow, it's worth checking your own Search Console API responses directly rather than relying on a single third-party date.

What stays consistent across every source covering this, including Google's own long-standing documentation on structured data generally: the removal is specifically of the visible SERP feature — the expandable snippet in the results page. It is not a removal, deprecation, or penalty against the underlying FAQPage schema markup itself.

This is worth repeating plainly because it's the single most common point of confusion in coverage of this change: your FAQPage JSON-LD markup is not deprecated, is not penalized, and does not need to be ripped out of your site. Google's standing guidance has always been that unused structured data does not negatively affect a page's ranking or its eligibility for other Search features. What's gone is the visual payoff — the expandable Q&A box in the results page itself.

Why this matters even though most sites lost the feature back in 2023

If FAQ rich results were already unavailable to nearly every ordinary business website after 2023, it's fair to ask why the 2026 completion is worth writing about at all. A few real reasons:

First, a lot of small-business owners and even some marketers never fully absorbed the 2023 restriction, and kept hoping that marking up their FAQ section with schema would eventually "unlock" the rich snippet again if their site grew more authoritative. The 2026 completion removes any ambiguity: there is no tier of authority, however large a business gets, that brings this specific feature back. It's not a site-quality problem to solve — the feature itself no longer exists for anyone.

Second, website audits and SEO checklists circulating online — some written years ago and never updated — still routinely list "add FAQPage schema for rich results" as an action item, implying there's a visible SERP reward waiting on the other side of implementing it. As of 2026, that specific promise is simply false for every site on the web, and continuing to sell clients on FAQ schema as a rich-result play is selling something that no longer exists. One SEO commentary piece on the change (SearchAtlas) frames this directly as Google reasserting control over how answers are displayed in its own results, rather than letting any publisher's markup dictate what the answer box looks like.

Third, and most practically: if your analytics or Search Console reporting ever showed impressions or clicks attributed to a FAQ rich result appearance, that specific attribution source is gone from your data going forward. Any historical reporting comparing periods before and after May 2026 needs to account for that, rather than reading a drop in FAQ-rich-result-driven clicks as a ranking problem with your site.

What this does not change

It's worth being precise about the boundaries of this change, because overreacting to it is as much of a mistake as ignoring it:

It does not affect your rankings. Losing a rich result is a loss of a specific visual presentation in the search results, not a ranking signal. A page that ranked well before May 2026 ranks on the same merits after it; it just can't show the expandable Q&A box anymore, and neither can any competitor.

It does not mean structured data is becoming less important generally. Coverage of this change is consistent that it's routine cleanup of one specific, narrowly-eligible feature — not a signal that Google is moving away from structured data as a whole. Other rich result types (Product, Recipe, Review, LocalBusiness, and more) continue to function independently of this change.

It does not mean FAQ-style content stops being useful to write. This is the point most worth internalizing for a small business: writing genuinely clear, specific answers to the real questions your customers ask is still valuable — for human readers deciding whether to call you, and increasingly for AI answer engines like Google's AI Overviews, Perplexity, and ChatGPT, which read and can cite well-structured Q&A content on a page regardless of whether that page also carries FAQPage JSON-LD or whether Google's SERP shows an expandable snippet for it. The content itself was never actually the thing that was removed — only one specific visual wrapper for it in one specific place.

What to actually do about it

For a small business or an agency managing a client's site, the practical response is almost entirely about discipline rather than urgency:

Leave existing FAQPage schema in place. There's no SEO or technical reason to remove it. Ripping out markup that's doing no harm, purely because the visible payoff is gone, creates unnecessary work and risk (a bad find-and-replace across templated pages has broken more sites than it's helped) for zero actual benefit.

Stop pitching FAQ schema as a rich-result growth tactic. If "we'll add FAQ schema to get you the expandable snippet in Google" is still part of an SEO pitch deck or proposal template anywhere, that line needs to come out now — it describes a feature that doesn't exist anymore for any site.

Keep writing real FAQ content, for the right reason. A clear, well-organized FAQ section that actually answers what customers ask — pricing questions, process questions, "do you serve my area" questions — is still worth having on a service page. Just hold it to the standard of "does this genuinely help a reader or an AI answer engine understand my business," not "will this earn a rich snippet," because the second standard no longer applies to anyone.

Re-baseline any reporting that included FAQ rich result metrics. If a client dashboard, an internal report, or a Search Console view ever tracked FAQ-specific impressions, note the May 2026 cutoff explicitly so a post-cutoff drop isn't misread as a ranking or indexing problem.

Frequently asked questions

Did Google remove FAQ rich results from search?

Yes. Reporting on Google's own documentation update says FAQ rich results stopped appearing in Google Search as of May 7, 2026 — the expandable question-and-answer snippet no longer shows for any site, including the small group of government and health sites that had kept eligibility since 2023.

Is FAQPage structured data now deprecated or harmful to use?

No. Only the visible search-results feature was removed. Google's own long-standing guidance is that unused structured data does not affect a site's ranking or Search presence, so leaving FAQPage markup in place is not a penalty risk — it simply no longer produces the rich snippet it used to.

Should I remove FAQPage schema from my website?

There's no requirement to remove it, but since it no longer earns a visible rich result, it's reasonable to stop treating FAQ schema as an SEO growth lever and instead prioritize writing genuinely clear on-page Q&A content for readers and AI answer engines, which don't depend on the now-retired Google SERP feature.
