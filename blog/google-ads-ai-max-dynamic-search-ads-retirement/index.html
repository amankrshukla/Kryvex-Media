Google Is Retiring Dynamic Search Ads for AI Max: What Small Business Advertisers Need to Know

Google announced in April 2026 that AI Max for Search will replace three legacy Search campaign settings — Dynamic Search Ads, automatically created assets, and campaign-level broad match — with eligible campaigns beginning to migrate automatically starting September 2026. For any small business running Google Ads, that means a chunk of campaign configuration most advertisers never touch after initial setup is about to be rewritten by Google's own system, whether or not anyone logs in and reviews it first. Here's what's actually changing, where reporting disagrees on timing, and what to check in an account before the automatic migration reaches it.

K
Kryvex Media Team
Oct 29, 2026 · 10 min read

Google confirmed in April 2026 that it is retiring three long-standing Search campaign settings — Dynamic Search Ads (DSA), automatically created assets, and campaign-level broad match — in favor of a single feature set called AI Max for Search. Multiple outlets covering the announcement (ppc.land, TechWyse, Search Engine Land) attribute it to Brandon Ervin, a Director of Product Management on the Google Ads and Commerce Blog, and report that advertisers will lose the ability to create new DSA campaigns going forward while eligible existing campaigns move to AI Max automatically starting in September 2026. For a small business that set up a DSA campaign years ago and never revisited it, this isn't an optional upgrade to consider — it's a change arriving on Google's timeline, with real decisions worth making before it lands.

What Dynamic Search Ads actually did, and why Google is retiring it

Dynamic Search Ads let an advertiser skip manually chosen keywords entirely: Google would crawl a website's existing pages and automatically generate ads and headlines targeting whatever search terms it judged relevant to that content. It was popular with small businesses specifically because it required very little ongoing management — no keyword list to maintain, no new ad copy to write for new products or services. "Automatically created assets" and "campaign-level broad match" were two related automation settings that let Google similarly expand targeting and generate ad components on its own within a standard Search campaign. According to reporting on Google's announcement, all three are being folded into AI Max, which Google frames as a more capable, more current version of the same underlying idea: let Google's systems handle keyword expansion and asset generation, but as a single integrated feature rather than three separate legacy settings.

The timeline — and where the reporting actually disagrees

Here's the part worth being precise about, because secondary coverage of this announcement doesn't fully agree. Search Engine Land's coverage describes eligible Search campaigns beginning to migrate to AI Max automatically starting in September 2026, with Google aiming to complete the rollout by the end of that month. A separate TechWyse report, published after the original announcement, describes Google extending the runway for advertisers who haven't manually upgraded their DSA campaigns, citing a later date — February 2027 — before an automatic transition reaches those specific campaigns, while adding that automatically created assets and campaign-level broad match settings were still expected to transition on the original September 2026 schedule regardless. Industry trade outlet ppc.land, covering advertiser reaction, reports pushback from some advertisers over the pace of the change, which may be part of why a reprieve was reported at all. Given that disagreement between sources, the responsible takeaway isn't a single date — it's that automatic migration is coming within this general window, and the one authoritative way to know an account's actual timeline is to check for Google's own upgrade prompts and migration notices directly inside that account's Google Ads interface.

Secondary coverage disagrees on the exact cutoff date for DSA campaigns specifically (September 2026 versus a later February 2027 reprieve). What's consistent across every source: new DSA campaign creation is going away, and Google — not the advertiser — controls exactly when any given existing campaign gets migrated.

AI Max is not Performance Max — a distinction worth getting right

Because "AI Max" and "Performance Max" sound similar and both lean on Google's automation, it's an easy mix-up worth clearing up directly. Performance Max is a separate campaign type that spans multiple Google inventory sources at once — Search, Display, YouTube, Discover, Gmail, and Maps — from a single campaign built around conversion goals and asset groups. AI Max for Search, by contrast, is a set of features applied within an existing, ordinary Search campaign: it's specifically about expanding keyword matching and generating additional headlines and descriptions for search-only inventory, not about running the same budget across every Google surface. An advertiser who already has Performance Max campaigns running isn't automatically affected by this specific DSA-to-AI-Max transition; this change is about what happens inside standard Search campaigns.

Google's own framing — and the number worth treating as a self-reported figure

Reporting on the announcement cites a performance claim from Google itself: that AI Max adoption has driven a 7% increase in Search campaign conversions across advertisers already using it, and that the feature left its beta phase after adoption by what TechWyse describes as hundreds of thousands of advertisers. Both figures are worth treating exactly as what they are — Google's own reported numbers about its own product, not an independently verified study — the same way this blog treats any platform's self-reported performance claim. Google has also framed the change as specifically aimed at helping local and small businesses reach a broader, more qualified audience, citing features like more granular location-targeting filters and brand-preference controls as part of AI Max's toolset, which is a real, stated goal worth weighing against the loss of manual control that broader automation always trades away.

What this actually changes for a small business account

For an advertiser not currently running DSA campaigns at all, this change mostly affects "automatically created assets" and campaign-level broad match — settings many small business accounts have running without anyone having deliberately chosen them, since they're sometimes recommended as default optimizations inside the Google Ads interface. Reviewing which of a Google Ads account's campaigns currently have those settings enabled, and understanding what AI Max will generate and target once it replaces them, is worth doing before an automatic migration does it first. For an advertiser actively relying on a DSA campaign — common for ecommerce catalogs or large content sites where manually maintaining a keyword list for every page isn't practical — the stakes are higher: a reviewed, intentional move to AI Max ahead of the automatic deadline gives a chance to set location and brand-preference filters deliberately, rather than inheriting whatever default configuration an automatic migration applies.

Mistakes worth avoiding right now

  • Assuming "I don't run DSA" means this doesn't affect my account. Campaign-level broad match and automatically created assets are also being folded into AI Max, and those settings are common in standard Search campaigns that were never explicitly labeled as Dynamic Search Ads.
  • Waiting for the automatic migration instead of checking the account now. Google Ads shows upgrade prompts and eligibility status directly inside the interface — that's the one authoritative source for a specific account's actual timeline, given that secondary reporting disagrees on exact dates.
  • Treating Google's 7% conversion-increase figure as an independently verified result. It's a real, reported number, but it's Google's own self-reported figure about its own product, not a third-party study — useful context, not proof of what will happen in any specific account.
  • Confusing AI Max for Search with Performance Max. They're different systems covering different inventory; conflating them can lead to reviewing the wrong campaign settings entirely.
  • Letting a DSA campaign built around an outdated site structure migrate automatically. If the website's page structure or product catalog has changed significantly since a DSA campaign was first set up, reviewing it manually before migration catches targeting that no longer matches what the business actually sells.

Where to actually start

Open the account and check specifically for any Dynamic Search Ads campaigns, then separately check standard Search campaigns for "automatically created assets" and campaign-level broad match settings under campaign settings — both are easy to have running without having deliberately turned them on. Look for Google's own upgrade or migration notices inside the interface rather than relying on an external date, since reporting on the rollout timeline doesn't fully agree. For any DSA campaign that's actually driving meaningful volume, review what page content it's currently targeting before an automatic migration carries that targeting into AI Max's broader keyword-matching system. And if location targeting or brand-safety controls matter for the business — a multi-location service business, or a brand protective of which search terms trigger its ads — set those filters deliberately inside AI Max rather than accepting whatever default settings an automatic migration applies.

Frequently asked questions

What is Google replacing Dynamic Search Ads with?

Google announced on its Ads and Commerce Blog in April 2026 that AI Max for Search is replacing three legacy Search campaign settings: Dynamic Search Ads (DSA), automatically created assets, and campaign-level broad match. AI Max uses Google's AI to generate additional headlines and descriptions and to broaden keyword matching automatically within a Search campaign, rather than requiring a separate DSA campaign type.

When will my Google Ads campaigns automatically move to AI Max?

Reporting on Google's announcement describes eligible Search campaigns beginning to migrate automatically starting September 2026, with Google aiming to complete migrations by the end of that month. Separate reporting describes Google extending the window for advertisers to manually upgrade Dynamic Search Ads campaigns, with one account of a later deadline citing February 2027 before an automatic transition applies to campaigns that haven't been manually upgraded. The exact date for any specific account is best confirmed inside Google Ads itself, since eligibility and timing are Google's own determination.

Is AI Max for Search just Performance Max for search campaigns?

No. AI Max is a feature set applied within an existing Search campaign, not a separate campaign type like Performance Max, which spans Search, Display, YouTube, Discover, Gmail, and Maps inventory from one campaign. AI Max expands keyword matching and generates additional ad assets specifically for Search campaigns, and reporting describes it as out of beta after adoption by a large number of advertisers, with Google citing an internal figure of a 7% increase in Search campaign conversions.
