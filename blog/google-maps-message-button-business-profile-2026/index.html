Google Maps Adds a Message Button Next to Call: What It Means for Your Business Profile

Google's November 2026 Small Business Bulletin confirms that Business Profiles with chat set up now show a Message button sitting directly next to the Call button on Google Maps, on both Android and iOS. Messages route straight to WhatsApp or SMS. It sounds small. For a lot of local businesses it's a genuinely different way customers will try to reach you starting now, and whether you're ready for it depends entirely on one setting most owners never touch.

K
Kryvex Media Team
Nov 12, 2026 · 9 min read

Google's Small Business Bulletin for November 2026 confirms a real, live change to how Business Profiles appear on Google Maps: a Message button now sits right next to the Call button on listings that have chat enabled, on both Android and iOS. Search Engine Roundtable's coverage quotes Google describing it directly as a prominent button where customers can message a listing without leaving Maps, with those messages routing to WhatsApp or SMS depending on how the profile's messaging is set up. If you manage a Google Business Profile and haven't looked at your messaging settings recently, this is the moment to check them, because the button's visibility depends entirely on a setting that's easy to forget exists.

What actually changed, and what hasn't

Before this update, Call was effectively the default fast action on a Business Profile listing in Maps, the one button every searcher saw first regardless of whether a business had anything else configured. Message existed as a feature, but it wasn't presented with the same prominence next to Call for every eligible listing the way it apparently now is. The bulletin frames this as a straightforward surfacing change: Google isn't introducing messaging to Business Profiles for the first time, it's making an existing capability far more visible to anyone browsing Maps, by putting it directly beside the button searchers already instinctively tap.

One detail matters more than it might look like at first glance. The Message button only shows up on profiles that already have chat set up. If your Business Profile doesn't have messaging turned on in your Google Business Profile settings, your listing keeps showing Call alone, the way it always has. You don't get the new button automatically just because Google rolled the feature out broadly. The feature is live; your eligibility for it isn't.

Why the destination matters as much as the button itself

Where a message actually lands once a customer sends it is the part worth paying closest attention to. Per the bulletin's description, messages sent through this button route to WhatsApp or SMS, not to a separate Google only inbox you'd need to check in yet another dashboard. That's a genuinely practical design choice, since it means the messages arrive in channels a lot of small businesses, especially ones in markets like India where WhatsApp is already the default customer communication tool, are already checking regularly as part of daily operations.

It also means the usual rules of fast response apply here exactly as they do to phone calls. A customer who taps Message instead of Call is choosing a channel precisely because it feels lower friction and less immediate than a phone call, but that doesn't mean they're expecting to wait a day for a reply. An unanswered WhatsApp message sitting for hours creates the same bad impression an unanswered phone call does, just through a different interface. If your team doesn't currently treat WhatsApp or SMS as a monitored customer service channel with the same urgency as your business line, this update is a reason to start.

Two other items appear in the same November bulletin and related coverage, worth knowing about but with less certainty behind them. A Gemini integration for Business Profile is reportedly rolling out to Google Workspace accounts, letting owners analyze performance data and draft review responses through chat commands, but Google's own help documentation still lists work accounts as excluded, so the two sources don't fully agree. Separately, one outlet reports call history for merchants is launching in the US and Canada for verified profiles, though the exact timing wasn't confirmed in that coverage. Treat both as developing, not settled, and check your own account directly rather than assuming either applies to you yet.

What this means if local search is where your customers find you

For service businesses, restaurants, clinics, and retail locations that live or die by local discovery, Call has long been the single most tracked and optimized action on a Business Profile. Adding a second, equally prominent fast action changes the actual customer journey, not just the visual layout of the listing. Some searchers, particularly younger ones and anyone used to messaging a business on Instagram or WhatsApp already, will now default to Message over Call without a second thought, simply because it's sitting right there with the same visual weight.

That's a real shift in how leads reach you, and it means a business that's spent years training staff to handle phone inquiries well but has never set up or monitored business messaging is now leaving a visible, Google promoted door half open. The fix isn't complicated. It's making sure chat is actually turned on, that whoever receives those WhatsApp or SMS messages treats them as real inbound leads, and that your team has a simple process for responding quickly, the same way a good business already has one for its phone line.

What this changes for businesses that already rely on WhatsApp

In markets where WhatsApp is already the default way customers talk to a business, this update closes a gap that's existed for a while between how people actually prefer to communicate and how Google's listing surfaced that option. A customer who'd normally search your business name, find your WhatsApp number separately listed somewhere on your website or a social bio, and then open WhatsApp manually can now do the whole thing from inside Maps itself, in one tap, right where they were already looking at your hours and reviews. That's a real reduction in friction between discovery and contact, and it's worth treating as a genuine opportunity rather than a cosmetic UI change, provided the chat setting behind it is actually turned on and someone is watching for replies.

What to actually do about this

Check whether chat is enabled on your Google Business Profile today, not assumed. Log into Business Profile settings directly and confirm messaging is turned on. If it isn't, you don't have the Message button showing next to Call on your listing, no matter how the rollout is described in press coverage.

Decide who actually monitors the WhatsApp or SMS thread this routes to, and how fast they're expected to respond. Treat this the same way you'd treat an unanswered incoming call: assign clear ownership rather than letting it become an inbox nobody checks regularly.

Don't assume the Gemini integration is live on your account just because it's in the bulletin. Given the conflict between the bulletin and Google's own help page, check your actual account settings before building any workflow around it.

If you serve the US or Canada and have a verified profile, keep an eye out for call history rolling out, but don't wait on it. The messaging change is the confirmed, actionable item here. Treat anything else in this bulletin as worth watching, not something to plan around yet.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to do anything to get the Message button on my Business Profile?

Yes. Per Google's own description of the feature as covered by Search Engine Roundtable's November 2026 Small Business Bulletin coverage, the Message button only appears on Business Profiles that have chat set up. If your profile doesn't have messaging enabled in Google Business Profile settings, your listing keeps showing only the Call button, not Message alongside it.

Where do messages sent through this button actually go?

To WhatsApp or SMS, depending on how your Business Profile's messaging is configured. This isn't a brand new inbox you need to separately monitor inside Google's own systems. It routes into channels your team may already be using, which is also why response time matters: a message sitting unanswered in a WhatsApp or SMS thread looks exactly as bad to a customer as an unanswered phone call.

Is the Gemini integration for Business Profile available to every business?

Not necessarily yet, and the available sources actually conflict here. The November 2026 bulletin states the feature is rolling out to Google Workspace accounts, but Google's own help documentation (answer 17142585) still lists work accounts as excluded at the time of that bulletin. Treat the Gemini rollout as in progress and inconsistent rather than universally live, and check your own account's actual settings rather than assuming access either way.
