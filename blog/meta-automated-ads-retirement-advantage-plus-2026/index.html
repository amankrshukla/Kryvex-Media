Meta Is Retiring Automated Ads — What Actually Replaces It

Meta is phasing out Automated Ads, the guided setup tool that let a business launch its first Facebook or Instagram campaign by answering a short set of questions rather than building targeting and creative from scratch. Trade coverage of the announcement is consistent on the direction — Advantage+ becomes the single path forward — but less precise on an exact shutdown date. Here's what's actually confirmed, what isn't, and what to do with a currently-running Automated Ads campaign.

K
Kryvex Media Team
Nov 5, 2026 · 8 min read

Automated Ads was built to solve a specific, real problem: a small business owner with no advertising background who wanted to run a Facebook or Instagram campaign but had no idea where to start with audience targeting, creative formats, or budget pacing. It let that owner answer a handful of guided questions about their goal — more website visits, more messages, more page likes — and have Meta assemble the campaign automatically from there. Trade coverage including Social Media Today and Mobile Marketing Reads reported in late 2025 that Meta is moving away from that tool specifically, framing it as a consolidation: Advantage+, Meta's broader AI-driven campaign system, is being positioned as the single recommended path rather than one of several entry points into Ads Manager.

What's actually confirmed, and what isn't

It's worth separating the parts of this story that different outlets agree on from the parts where the reporting gets vaguer, because the vague parts matter if you're deciding when to act. What's consistent across coverage: Meta is removing Automated Ads as an option for creating new campaigns, existing Automated Ads campaigns that are already running continue to operate for the time being, and the stated reason is that Advantage+ already does the same job — guided, AI-assisted setup — with more capability layered on top, making a separate, simpler tool redundant rather than necessary. What's less precise: the exact date full removal takes effect. Different outlets describe the timeline as "over the coming months," "removed as an option... over the next few months," and "completely removed in 2026," which point in the same general direction without agreeing on a specific date. Rather than state a precise shutdown date that no single source actually confirms, the honest summary is: the direction is settled, the exact calendar date isn't, as of this writing.

One sometimes-confused, separate change worth distinguishing: Meta's Marketing API version 25.0 deprecated the ability to create or update Advantage+ Shopping Campaigns and Advantage+ App Campaigns through older API paths, with that restriction extending across all API versions after May 19, 2026. That's a developer-facing API change about how campaigns of a specific type get created programmatically — it is not the same announcement as the Automated Ads retirement in Ads Manager's user interface, even though both push advertisers in the same general direction, toward Advantage+ as the standard.

If you currently run an Automated Ads campaign, deleting it ends the plan and makes its historical results inaccessible, according to reporting on how the feature behaves — a detail worth knowing before clicking delete on anything, since it means exporting whatever performance data you want to keep before removing the campaign, not after.

What Advantage+ actually offers instead

For a small business that liked Automated Ads specifically for its simplicity, the honest pitch for Advantage+ is that it keeps the same core idea — describe your goal, let Meta's systems handle targeting and placement — while adding real capability Automated Ads never had. Advantage+ campaigns pull from a broader set of Meta's machine-learning targeting signals, support more granular budget and bidding controls when you want them, and report performance with more depth than Automated Ads' simplified view provided. The tradeoff, if there is one, is that Advantage+'s setup flow surfaces more decisions along the way than Automated Ads' minimal question set did — which is a reasonable cost for a business that's grown past needing the absolute simplest version of guided setup, and arguably no real cost at all for a business working with an agency that handles that setup on their behalf.

It's also worth noting that Advantage+ itself hasn't been static — Meta has continued layering updates onto it, including expanded creative tools and changes to how placement budget gets allocated across the various automated options. That's a separate, ongoing stream of changes from the Automated Ads retirement specifically, but it's part of why Meta frames Advantage+ as the more future-proof destination: it's the product actively being developed and improved, while Automated Ads was, by Meta's own framing, treated as redundant precisely because Advantage+ had already caught up to and surpassed what it originally offered.

Part of a bigger pattern of Meta simplifying Ads Manager

Automated Ads' retirement doesn't sit in isolation — it's one piece of a broader direction Meta has been pushing Ads Manager toward over the past couple of years: fewer manual levers, more decisions handed to its own automated systems, and a smaller number of campaign types that cover more use cases each. Trade press covering related changes around the same period reported Meta also removing some detailed manual targeting options in Ads Manager, pushing advertisers further toward the broader audience signals its AI-driven systems use instead of granular interest- or behavior-based targeting lists an advertiser used to build by hand. Separately, Meta has also been consolidating its machine-learning ad offerings — moving Advantage+ shopping campaigns toward a rename as Advantage+ sales campaigns, and testing an even more unified, single Advantage+ campaign setup flow that would eventually fold in objectives that currently exist as separate choices.

The common thread across all of these changes, including the Automated Ads retirement, is that Meta is betting its own prediction systems make better targeting and budget-allocation decisions at scale than a human manually configuring narrow settings — a bet that's genuinely controversial among performance marketers, some of whom argue it removes useful control precisely when an advertiser's own first-party data or niche audience knowledge would outperform a generic automated signal. Whichever side of that debate you land on, the practical reality for anyone running ads on Facebook or Instagram is the same: the range of manual, DIY options is shrinking campaign type by campaign type, and Advantage+ is where Meta is consolidating nearly everything that remains.

For a small business owner weighing whether this actually matters to them, the honest answer depends on how hands-on their current setup is. A business that set up an Automated Ads campaign years ago and hasn't touched it since will barely notice the transition if it's handled proactively, since Advantage+ covers the same basic use case with more capability underneath. A business or agency that was specifically relying on Automated Ads' narrower, simpler question set — precisely because it intentionally avoided the deeper configuration options Advantage+ exposes — will feel the change more, since there's no equivalently minimal setup path left once Automated Ads is gone. That's a smaller group of advertisers than it sounds, since most agencies managing Meta Ads professionally, including Kryvex Media, were already defaulting new client campaigns to Advantage+ rather than Automated Ads well before this retirement was announced, precisely because of the reporting depth difference described above.

What to actually do if you're running Automated Ads today

The practical move is neither panic nor inaction. Reporting on the change specifically advises against waiting for Automated Ads to simply disappear — the better approach is to rebuild the same campaign logic inside Advantage+ now, while you still have your Automated Ads campaign's historical results available side by side to compare against. That gives you a real before-and-after read on whether the migration actually changes performance for your specific business, rather than switching cold once the old tool is gone and losing the ability to compare. For a business that set up its original Automated Ads campaign once, years ago, and hasn't touched the settings since, this is also a reasonable natural checkpoint to review targeting and creative that may be stale regardless of which tool runs it.

Frequently asked questions

What was Meta's Automated Ads feature?

Automated Ads was a guided setup tool inside Meta Ads Manager that let a business launch a Facebook or Instagram campaign by answering a short set of questions about its goals, with Meta then assembling the creative and targeting automatically.

Is Meta removing Automated Ads immediately?

No. Reporting on the announcement describes a phased removal: new Automated Ads campaigns are being phased out as an option in Ads Manager over time, while campaigns already running continue to operate for the time being.

What should I use instead of Automated Ads?

Advantage+ campaigns are Meta's current recommended path and the direct successor, offering similar AI-assisted setup with more control and reporting depth than Automated Ads provided.
