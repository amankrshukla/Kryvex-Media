Google Local Services Ads Is Changing Three Ways in 2026: What To Check

If you run Local Services Ads for a home-service business, three separate things about the product changed in the back half of 2026: Google started billing for some missed calls on October 1, it quietly renamed the policies that govern your account in July, and, as the big one, it's folding the entire standalone LSA dashboard into regular Google Ads starting in August, in phases that run into 2027. None of these are rumors; they're documented in Google's own advertiser notices and confirmed across PPC Land, Search Engine Land, MediaPost, and Search Engine Roundtable. Here's what each one actually does.

Local Services Ads (LSA), the pay-per-lead listings with the green "Google Screened" or "Google Guaranteed" badge that plumbers, electricians, HVAC techs, and other home-service businesses use to show up above the Local Pack, has had an unusually eventful second half of 2026. Three separate changes landed within about four months of each other: a billing change that affects what you pay for, a policy-document rename that's mostly cosmetic, and a platform migration that is not cosmetic at all. Advertisers who only skim one notice and assume that's the whole story are likely to miss at least one of the other two.

Change #1: Google will now charge you for some calls nobody answers

This is the one with real dollar impact, and it took effect October 1, 2026. Per Google's advertiser notice, summarized consistently by Search Engine Land, PPC Land, TechWyse, and Search Engine Roundtable, a missed call during your stated business hours now counts as a billable, valid lead if the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds, with a few stated exceptions. Previously, an unanswered call generally wasn't something you paid for; the logic now is that a caller who holds for over 20 seconds is demonstrating real intent to reach you, whether or not the call actually connects.

There's a second piece that matters just as much: repeat calls. Google's Ads Liaison, Ginny Marvin, clarified publicly that if the same customer calls back again within 15 days of that first interaction, you're only charged once for the pair, not charged again for every follow-up attempt from someone who's already generated a billable lead. That's a meaningful protection against being billed multiple times for one customer who simply couldn't get through on the first try and called back later the same day.

What's genuinely unresolved, and flagged by multiple outlets covering the rollout, is exactly when Google's 20-second clock starts: at ring start, at pickup-attempt, or at some other point in the call flow. That ambiguity matters in practice: a call-tracking platform may log both a quick disconnect and a 20-plus-second hold under the same "missed call" label, which can make it genuinely hard to audit your own bill against what actually happened on the line unless your call-tracking setup distinguishes hold duration specifically.

Change #2: a policy rename that's mostly paperwork (July 2026)

Separately, and considerably smaller in real-world impact, Google renamed what used to be called the "Local Services platform policies" to "Local Services Ads requirements," per Search Engine Roundtable's coverage, and cleaned up several legacy policy entries that no longer applied to the current product. Sources differ slightly on the exact effective date within the notice itself: one reading gives July 6, another gives July 31, so treat the precise day as unconfirmed, but the substance is consistent across coverage: this is a wording and structure cleanup, not a new rule, and there's no indication existing advertisers face a fresh re-verification requirement because of it. Worth knowing it happened, mainly so you don't mistake a July policy-page change for something that affects your account today.

Change #3: Local Services Ads is being folded into Google Ads, and this is the real one

This is the change that actually restructures how you manage LSA campaigns, and it's rolling out in phases starting August 2026, continuing into 2027. Per reporting from PPC Land, MediaPost, and several PPC-focused trade outlets, Google is retiring the standalone LSA dashboard entirely for migrated accounts. Once your account migrates, logging into the old LSA interface simply redirects you into a campaign inside regular Google Ads, specifically a specialized form of Performance Max built around a pay-per-lead goal rather than the usual conversion-value goals Performance Max normally optimizes toward.

What reportedly survives the move: pay-per-lead billing itself, your placements in Search and Google Maps, keywordless targeting (you don't pick keywords in LSA; Google matches you to relevant local searches), and the Google Verified or Google Screened badge that's the whole reason many home-service businesses run LSA in the first place. What reportedly does not survive: your historical performance reports and the dashboard itself. If you lean on year-over-year comparisons, seasonal benchmarking, or client-facing performance reporting built from the LSA dashboard's own data, the guidance from several of the outlets covering this is blunt: export everything before your account's migration date, because it won't be retrievable from the old interface afterward.

The phase-in reportedly starts with a specific group: select U.S. advertisers across nine trades (plumbing, HVAC, electrical, appliance repair, house cleaning, lawn care, roofing, pest control, and moving) migrate first. Later phases reportedly extend to service-area businesses without a physical storefront and accounts running custom bidding or booking integrations, with non-U.S. accounts and any remaining categories not covered in 2026 pushed into a 2027 phase. Account administrators are reportedly notified by email 14 days ahead of their specific migration date, with in-dashboard warning banners and a second reminder email seven days out. If you haven't seen that email yet, it doesn't mean you're exempt. It likely means your trade or market simply hasn't been scheduled yet.

One other change got bundled into some coverage of this same migration window and is worth separating out clearly: Local Inventory Ads, a different Google product that surfaces nearby retail stock inside Shopping campaigns, was separately reported to be switching on by default starting August 31, 2026. That's not an LSA change at all; if you don't run Shopping campaigns with physical retail inventory, it doesn't apply to you, and conflating the two (as a couple of headlines do) is a real source of confusion worth avoiding.

A manual-bidding detail worth flagging for anyone who's hands-on with their LSA budget: the migration is also reported to retire manual bidding and vertical-level cost targets in favor of a single campaign-level target, and weekly budgets reportedly convert to a daily average. If you've been managing bids trade-by-trade or week-by-week inside the old dashboard, that workflow doesn't carry over as-is. Budget for a transition period where you re-learn the controls, not an instant like-for-like swap.

What to actually do about all this right now

Export your LSA historical reports today, not when the migration email arrives. Given the reporting that the old dashboard and its data won't survive the move, doing this proactively, rather than racing the 14-day countdown once it starts, protects any client reporting or seasonal comparisons you rely on.

Tighten your call-tracking setup around hold duration specifically, not just "answered vs. missed." Since the 20-second threshold is where billing now kicks in for unanswered calls, a call-tracking platform that distinguishes a 5-second hangup from a 25-second hold gives you something real to audit your bill against, rather than taking Google's lead count on faith.

Watch for the 14-day migration email, and don't assume no email means no change coming. If your trade is one of the nine named in the first phase (plumbing, HVAC, electrical, appliance repair, house cleaning, lawn care, roofing, pest control, moving), budget time to review the new Performance Max-based interface before historical data potentially becomes unavailable.

Don't confuse this with the Local Inventory Ads default-on change. If a vendor or headline tells you "your local ads just changed" without specifying which product, check whether they mean LSA (pay-per-lead, service businesses) or Local Inventory Ads (retail Shopping stock) before you act on it.

Frequently asked questions

Will I get charged for every missed call on Local Services Ads now?

Only some. Per Google's advertiser notice, as reported by Search Engine Land, PPC Land, and other outlets, a missed call during your stated business hours becomes a billable lead if the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds, with a few stated exceptions. Google's Ads Liaison, Ginny Marvin, clarified that a follow-up call from the same customer within 15 days of that first interaction is only charged once, not again each time they call back. The notice doesn't spell out exactly when Google's 20-second clock starts counting, which several outlets have flagged as a real open question for advertisers trying to audit their own billing.

Is Local Services Ads being merged into regular Google Ads?

Yes, in phases starting August 2026, according to reporting from PPC Land, MediaPost, and several PPC-focused outlets. Migrated accounts lose their standalone LSA dashboard: logging in redirects to a specialized Performance Max campaign inside Google Ads. Pay-per-lead billing, Search and Maps placements, keywordless targeting, and the Google Verified/Screened badge are reported to carry over, but historical performance reports and the old dashboard itself reportedly do not transfer, so advertisers are being told to export their records before their account's migration date arrives.

Is this the same thing as the Local Inventory Ads change?

No. These are two different Google ad products that happened to change around the same time, which has caused some confusion in coverage. Local Services Ads is the pay-per-lead product mainly used by home-service trades like plumbing and HVAC. Local Inventory Ads is a Shopping-campaign feature that surfaces nearby retail stock, separately reported to be turned on by default starting August 31, 2026. If you don't run Shopping campaigns, the Local Inventory Ads change doesn't apply to you.
