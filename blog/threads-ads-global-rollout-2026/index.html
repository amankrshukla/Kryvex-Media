Threads Ads Go Global: What Small Businesses Need to Know in 2026

Threads spent its first two years as a text-based app with almost no advertising. That changed through 2026: Meta expanded Threads ads to every user worldwide, added new formats, and folded Threads management into the same Ads Manager and Business Settings used for Facebook and Instagram — all while the app itself kept growing. Here's what the real rollout timeline, user numbers, and ad formats look like, and how a small business should realistically think about testing the channel.

K
Kryvex Media Team
Nov 3, 2026 · 9 min read

For most of its early life, Threads was a growth story without a monetization story. Meta launched the text-based app in mid-2023 as a fast-moving answer to X (formerly Twitter), it grew quickly in bursts, and advertising stayed limited to small tests for a long stretch after launch. 2026 is the year that changed on two fronts at once: the user base kept climbing toward a genuine mass-scale audience, and Meta finally turned on advertising for everyone, not just a test group. For a small business weighing whether Threads deserves a place in the paid-social mix, both of those facts matter, and the real data on timing, scale, and format is worth separating from the marketing-blog noise around it.

The rollout: from a small test to every user, worldwide

Meta's approach to Threads ads followed a familiar cautious pattern before 2026: a limited test with a small group of advertisers, reported as starting in the US and Japan, expanding gradually through 2025 as Meta built out targeting and measurement. The real turning point came at the start of 2026. TechCrunch reported on January 21, 2026 that Meta was rolling ads out to all Threads users worldwide, with the broader expansion beginning the following week; the same reporting noted the rollout was expected to take months to fully complete, with ad delivery staying intentionally low at first as Meta scaled the system up. That's a meaningfully different posture from the earlier test phase — a committed, global default rather than an invite-only pilot — and it's consistent with how Meta has rolled out ad products on other surfaces in the past, where a slow, capacity-managed expansion follows the initial "ads are now available" announcement rather than everything turning on at once.

On the infrastructure side, Meta's own developer documentation describes Threads accounts being folded into the same Business Settings interface already used for Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp. Instagram-associated Threads accounts created before January 29, 2026 were reportedly added to a business's Business Portfolio automatically; accounts created after that date need to be added manually. Practically, that means a business already running Meta ads doesn't need a separate login or a new account structure to start testing Threads — it shows up alongside the other Meta surfaces inside the same Ads Manager, and third-party brand-safety verification through Meta Business Partners, previously available on Facebook and Instagram, was extended to the Threads feed as part of the same rollout.

How big is the audience now

The scale question has a real, Meta-sourced answer, and it moved fast through 2026. Meta had reported Threads passing 400 million monthly active users in August 2025. By June 16, 2026, multiple reports of Meta's own announcement put that figure at 500 million monthly active users — a genuine jump within roughly ten months, and one that happened specifically during the window when global ads were rolling out, not before it. Separately, TechCrunch reported in January 2026 that Threads had edged out X in daily mobile users according to third-party usage data; the same period saw daily active user estimates reported inconsistently across trackers, with one figure citing daily actives topping 150 million and a separate Similarweb-based estimate putting iOS and Android daily actives at roughly 141.5 million as of January 7, 2026. Those two numbers don't agree exactly, which is normal for third-party app-usage trackers using different measurement methods and device coverage, but both land in the same rough range and tell the same basic story. None of this makes Threads a larger platform than Instagram or Facebook — it isn't, by a wide margin — but it does mean the audience is no longer small enough to dismiss as a niche text-app experiment. A platform with a genuine half-billion monthly users and a newly-global ad product is a different proposition than the same app was in 2024, when Threads launched as Meta's fast-built response to Twitter's acquisition-era turmoil and spent its first stretch of life focused purely on growth rather than monetization.

The advertiser-access timeline has its own, separately reported history worth being precise about, since sources describe it slightly differently depending on which phase they're covering. TechCrunch's coverage describes advertisers gaining broader access in April 2025 — a meaningfully larger step than the original US/Japan-only test but still short of the "every Threads user, worldwide" scope that arrived with the January 2026 announcement. A separate industry source dates a related expansion announcement to the same April 2025 window. Treat these as describing the same mid-2025 expansion phase from different angles rather than two separate events — the point that matters for a small business is that there were genuinely several distinct stages (a small 2024 test, a wider 2025 expansion, then the 2026 global default), not a single on/off switch, and each stage came with more advertiser tooling and a larger reachable audience than the one before it.

What ad formats are actually available

Coverage of the global rollout describes two newer formats joining what was already available from the earlier test phase: carousel ads, letting an advertiser show multiple images or products in a single swipeable unit, and Advantage+ catalog ads, which pull directly from a connected product catalog — the same format ecommerce advertisers already use heavily on Facebook and Instagram. Single-image and video ad formats, the backbone of the original test-phase rollout, remain available as well. Because campaign management runs through the same Ads Manager as the rest of Meta's ad surfaces, a business already running Advantage+ shopping campaigns on Facebook or Instagram can, in principle, extend an existing catalog-based campaign to include Threads as an additional placement rather than building a Threads-specific campaign from scratch.

Some figures circulating alongside this rollout aren't solid enough to repeat as fact. Claims that Threads ads run 30-40% cheaper than comparable Instagram placements, and a projected $11.3 billion in Threads ad revenue, both trace back to a single startup-focused blog without a primary source behind either number — treat them as unverified marketing chatter, not real benchmarks, until independent data backs them up.

How a small business should actually approach testing this

The honest case for testing Threads ads right now rests on two solid facts, not hype: the ad product is genuinely out of its invite-only test phase and running on the same infrastructure as the rest of Meta's ads, and the audience has grown to a real, half-billion-user scale rather than a speculative one. Neither fact guarantees performance for any specific business — a platform's total user count says very little about whether a specific audience, offer, or creative format will work on it, and Meta's own rollout commentary explicitly describes ad delivery as starting low while the system scales. The sensible approach mirrors how any new ad placement should be tested: a modest, clearly-bounded test budget — something in the range of 10-20% of an existing Instagram or Facebook budget, run for one to two weeks before judging results — rather than either ignoring the channel entirely or shifting a meaningful chunk of spend to it on day one. Because Threads runs through the same Ads Manager, campaign structure, and (where applicable) product catalog as existing Meta campaigns, the actual setup cost of running that test is close to zero for a business that's already advertising on Facebook or Instagram, which is the main practical reason this is worth a real test rather than something to defer indefinitely.

Frequently asked questions

When did Threads ads become available to all advertisers?

TechCrunch reported on January 21, 2026 that Meta was rolling ads out to all Threads users worldwide, with the expansion beginning the following week and expected to take months to fully complete. Before that, Meta had tested Threads ads with a limited group of advertisers starting in 2024, expanding gradually through 2025.

How many people use Threads in 2026?

Meta announced that Threads passed 500 million monthly active users on June 16, 2026, according to multiple reports of the announcement. That's up from the roughly 400 million monthly active users Meta had reported in August 2025.

What ad formats are available on Threads?

Reporting on the global rollout describes carousel ads and Advantage+ catalog ads being added to Threads alongside the single-image and video ad formats already supported from the earlier test phase. Threads accounts and ad delivery are managed through the same Meta Ads Manager and Business Settings used for Facebook and Instagram.
