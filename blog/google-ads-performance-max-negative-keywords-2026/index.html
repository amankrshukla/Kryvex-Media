Performance Max Isn't a Black Box Anymore: Negative Keywords & Search Term Reporting Explained

For most of its life, Performance Max was the Google Ads campaign type advertisers loved for results and distrusted for opacity — you couldn't see which search terms triggered your ads, and you couldn't block a single one of them. Google has now changed that: campaign-level negative keywords (up to 10,000 per campaign), full search term reporting, and channel-level performance breakdowns are documented, live features on Google's own Ads Help pages. Here's exactly what changed, what it still can't do, and how to actually use it.

K
Kryvex Media Team
Oct 31, 2026 · 9 min read

Google's own Google Ads Help documentation now confirms three controls that advertisers had asked for since Performance Max launched in 2021: campaign-level negative keywords (up to 10,000 per campaign), search term reporting (including through the API), and channel-level performance reporting. Industry coverage from PPC Land, MediaPost, and Search Engine Land tracked the rollout across 2025 and into 2026, and Google's support pages — "Negative keywords in Performance Max campaigns" and "Unlock more visibility and control in Performance Max" — now describe them as live, documented features rather than beta tests. For a campaign type that was, for years, criticized specifically for being an unauditable black box, that's a real shift in how much a small business advertiser can actually see and control.

What actually changed

Three specific things, each addressing a specific long-standing complaint:

  • Campaign-level negative keywords. You can now add up to 10,000 negative keywords directly inside a Performance Max campaign's settings, with Google also offering shared negative keyword lists you can apply across campaigns. This closes the single most-requested missing feature in PMax's history — previously, the only way to exclude unwanted traffic was at the account level, which meant every negative applied everywhere, whether you wanted it to or not.
  • Search term reporting. You can now see which actual search queries triggered your Performance Max ads, including through the Google Ads API for advertisers who pull reporting into their own dashboards. Before this, PMax was the only major Google Ads campaign type where you genuinely could not see what your ads were matching to.
  • Channel-level reporting. Google has also rolled out reporting that breaks Performance Max performance down by the channel it served on — Search, Shopping, Display, YouTube, Gmail, Discover, and Maps — so you can finally see roughly where your budget is actually being spent, not just the blended total.

Important scope limit: negative keywords in Performance Max apply only to Search and Shopping inventory inside that campaign. They do not block Display, YouTube, Gmail, or Discover placements — those still run on Google's automated targeting with no keyword-level exclusion available.

Why advertisers had been asking for this

Performance Max replaced Smart Shopping and several other legacy campaign types in 2021-2022, consolidating Search, Shopping, Display, YouTube, Gmail, Discover, and Maps inventory into one automated campaign that Google's machine learning manages end-to-end. The trade-off advertisers accepted was giving up granular control — no keyword-level bidding, no placement-level exclusions, no visibility into which queries or placements were actually converting — in exchange for Google's algorithm finding opportunities a human manager might miss across all those surfaces at once. That trade-off worked well for budget and conversion volume in a lot of accounts, but it created a real problem: when a PMax campaign wasted spend on clearly irrelevant traffic (a car dealership's ads showing for "toy car" searches, for example), there was no tool to stop it. Advertisers could pause the whole campaign, but not surgically fix the one bad match. That's the specific gap campaign-level negative keywords close.

What's still off-limits inside Performance Max

Worth being precise about this, because the new controls are genuinely useful but don't turn PMax into a Search campaign with extra channels bolted on:

  • No keyword-level bidding — you still can't raise or lower a bid for a specific search term the way you can in a Search campaign.
  • No placement-level exclusions for Display, YouTube, or Gmail inventory — negative keywords cover Search and Shopping only.
  • No manual control over which asset group serves on which channel — Google's automation still makes that call.
  • No audience-segment-level bid adjustment inside a single asset group.

The practical read: these updates give you visibility and the ability to block clearly wrong matches, not the granular steering wheel a Search campaign gives you. Treat PMax's new controls as a seatbelt, not a steering wheel.

How to actually use campaign-level negative keywords

  1. Pull your Performance Max search term report first — in-platform or via the API — before adding a single negative. Guessing at what to exclude defeats the purpose of finally having the data.
  2. Look specifically for queries that are topically unrelated to what you sell, not just queries with a low conversion rate — PMax's bidding algorithm is often still finding genuinely relevant traffic that simply hasn't converted yet in a short reporting window.
  3. Add negatives at the campaign level for anything clearly off-topic (the classic example: a plumbing company's ads matching "how to fix a leak yourself" DIY searches).
  4. Build a shared negative keyword list for terms that should be excluded account-wide (competitor brand names you don't want to bid on, for instance) rather than re-entering them campaign by campaign.
  5. Re-check the search term report every 1-2 weeks for the first month after adding negatives — this is new functionality for most advertiser accounts, and the first pass rarely catches everything worth excluding.

Who this actually helps most

Small businesses running a single Performance Max campaign against a tight product catalog or local service area benefit the most, because a single irrelevant match can represent a meaningful share of total spend when budgets are small. A local HVAC company running PMax with a $1,500/month budget loses far more, proportionally, to one bad keyword match than a national retailer running PMax at six figures a month. If your account has never had search term visibility before, the first audit after this rollout is worth doing properly rather than skimming — it's common to find at least a handful of clearly unrelated queries eating budget once you can actually see them.

What to actually check in your account this week

If you manage your own Performance Max campaigns rather than outsourcing them, here's the concrete checklist worth running once these controls are available on your account:

  • Pull the search term report for the last 30-60 days before touching any settings. You want a baseline of what's actually been happening, not a guess based on how the campaign "feels" like it's performing.
  • Sort by impressions and spend, not just by clicks. A query that's eating budget without converting is worth seeing even if it never generated a click-through you'd have noticed otherwise.
  • Check the channel-level breakdown to see whether your spend is concentrated where you'd expect (Search and Shopping, for a typical product-led business) or scattered more heavily into Display, Discover, or YouTube inventory than you'd assumed.
  • Resist over-excluding. The temptation with a brand-new tool is to aggressively negative-keyword anything unfamiliar. Performance Max's bidding algorithm is frequently still finding legitimately relevant long-tail queries that a human wouldn't have thought to target manually — cutting those off defeats part of what made the campaign type useful in the first place.

The broader pattern this fits into

This isn't an isolated change — it's part of a consistent pattern across Google Ads over the past two years: Google has been steadily adding transparency and exclusion tools back into its most heavily automated campaign types after advertiser pushback, without giving up the automation itself. The same logic shows up in how Google has handled other AI-driven ad products: keep the machine-learning targeting and bidding as the default, but add visibility and override options where advertisers can show the automation is making avoidable mistakes. For a small business owner, the practical takeaway is the same regardless of which specific feature rolls out next: automated campaign types are not "set and forget," and the moment a platform gives you new visibility into what's happening inside one, it's worth actually using it rather than letting the campaign keep running unchecked on the assumption that automation means no oversight is needed.

A reasonable next step

If your Performance Max campaigns have been running for months without anyone checking search term data (because, until recently, there wasn't any to check), that's a legitimate gap worth closing now that the reporting exists — not a sign anything was mismanaged before. Pulling the report, adding a first round of campaign-level negatives, and setting a recurring check-in is a concrete, low-risk improvement any account running Performance Max can make this month. This sits alongside the broader point covered in how to cut wasted ad spend in 30 days — visibility into where budget actually goes is the prerequisite for cutting waste, and for the first time, Performance Max advertisers genuinely have that visibility to act on.

Frequently asked questions

Can you add negative keywords to a Performance Max campaign?

Yes. Google has rolled campaign-level negative keywords out to Performance Max, documented on Google's own Google Ads Help pages. You can add up to 10,000 negative keywords per campaign, and they apply only to the Search and Shopping inventory inside that Performance Max campaign — they do not affect Display, YouTube, or Gmail placements, which Performance Max can also serve ads into.

Can you see what search terms triggered your Performance Max ads?

Yes, search term reporting for Performance Max campaigns is now available, including through the Google Ads API, according to Google's own support documentation. This closes one of the most criticized gaps in the original version of Performance Max, where advertisers couldn't see which queries their ads were actually matching.

Does Performance Max now give the same level of control as a Search campaign?

No. Even with negative keywords and search term reporting added, Performance Max remains an automated, asset-group-based campaign type — you still can't bid on individual keywords, control which specific placement or audience segment gets which ad, or split budget by channel the way a Search or Display campaign lets you. The new controls add visibility and exclusion ability, not the granular bid-and-placement control a traditional campaign type offers.
