Microsoft Advertising's November 2026 Overhaul. What Small Advertisers Need to Check.

Microsoft's November 2026 advertiser newsletter brought a campaign management interface overhaul already live in accounts, AI Max moving from a limited rollout to full availability, and a new report showing which landing page each search term actually routed to. One earlier October claim about Performance Max device targeting also quietly got walked back. Here is what actually changed, and what to look at in your own account before assuming any of it applies automatically.

K
Kryvex Media Team
Nov 14, 2026 · 9 min read

If you run paid search on Microsoft Advertising alongside Google Ads, your account interface looked different this month, and not by accident. Microsoft's November 2026 advertiser newsletter, published November 9, confirmed a campaign management interface overhaul that the company says is already live in accounts, not an upcoming change to prepare for. Alongside it came AI Max becoming generally available rather than a limited rollout, a genuinely new reporting view, and a correction to something Microsoft itself had said a month earlier. None of this is Google Ads news, but if Microsoft Advertising is part of your paid search mix, each of these items affects what you will actually see and need to configure.

The interface overhaul is already live, not upcoming

The headline item in the November newsletter is a campaign management interface overhaul that Microsoft says has already rolled out inside advertiser accounts. Coverage of the update also points to new target-versus-actual bid strategy reporting arriving alongside it, giving advertisers a clearer view of how a bid strategy's results compare against what it was actually set to target. If your account's navigation or reporting layout looks different than it did a few weeks ago, this is almost certainly why, and it's worth a few minutes clicking through the new layout rather than assuming a setting moved or disappeared.

AI Max is now generally available, and it comes with a real requirement

AI Max, which bundles predictive matching and autogenerated text assets under one feature set, is moving from a limited rollout to general availability across Microsoft Advertising accounts. Microsoft has stated that campaigns already using either predictive matching or autogenerated text assets individually will have the corresponding AI Max setting enabled automatically as part of this rollout, meaning you may not need to turn anything on yourself for it to start applying. The detail worth actually checking in your own account: turning on any AI Max feature requires conversion-based bidding. If a campaign is running on a different bid strategy, manual CPC for instance, AI Max features won't apply to it until that bid strategy changes, which is a real decision with its own tradeoffs, not something to switch casually just to access a newer feature set.

Microsoft has also said AI Max testers gained additional conversions during the limited rollout period. That figure comes from Microsoft's own testing and hasn't been independently verified against a specific account's results, so treat it as the company's stated testing outcome rather than a guaranteed result for your own campaigns.

A genuinely new report: search term to landing page mapping

Starting mid-November, Microsoft Advertising added a report showing which landing page each search term was actually routed to. Microsoft describes this as showing how user intent maps to the page a click actually lands on, which is a real, practical gap this closes. Search term reports have always shown what someone typed and whether it triggered a click; they haven't always made it obvious which of several possible landing pages that specific search term's traffic ended up on, especially in accounts running several ad groups or dynamic search ads pointed at different sections of a site. If a search term is converting poorly, this report is where to check whether it actually landed somewhere that matched its intent before assuming the keyword or the bid is the problem.

Performance Max device targeting: still a pilot, despite an earlier claim

Here's the part worth flagging directly: an October 28 Microsoft post had said device targeting for Performance Max would roll out to all advertisers in the coming weeks. The November newsletter instead describes it as still in pilot. That's not a minor wording difference, it's a walked-back timeline on a specific feature. If you were watching for broad Performance Max device targeting access based on that October announcement, it hasn't arrived yet as of this November update, and there's no new date attached to when it will. Audience exclusions, covering Customer Match and remarketing lists, are also still described as in pilot rather than generally available, and coverage of the newsletter has described that specific feature's status inconsistently between "open beta" and "open pilot" in different parts of the same write-up, which is itself a sign to check your own account's actual feature availability rather than rely on secondary summaries, including this one, for exactly which pilot tier you're in.

One bidding change that already took effect

Separately, and dated earlier than the November newsletter itself, Max CPC was removed as a bid strategy option for new non-portfolio campaigns starting October 1, 2026. Existing campaigns already running on Max CPC weren't necessarily forced off it by this change, but any new non-portfolio campaign created after that date would need a different bid strategy. If you or whoever manages your account creates campaigns directly in the interface rather than through a managed agency relationship, this is worth knowing before you go looking for an option that used to be there.

Why this matters even if Google Ads is your main channel

Microsoft Advertising reaches a meaningfully smaller and often different audience than Google Ads, and for most small businesses running paid search, Google Ads remains the larger channel by volume. That doesn't make Microsoft Advertising irrelevant: it often has a different competitive density and cost profile for the same keywords, which is exactly why some advertisers run both. If you already have a Microsoft Advertising account, this month's changes are worth a real look rather than skipping past them because your Google Ads account is where most of the budget sits. If you don't run Microsoft Advertising at all, none of this is a reason to start urgently, but it is a reasonable moment to ask whether your current paid search setup is actually using every channel that could reasonably reach your customers, or just the one that happened to get set up first.

What to actually check in your account this week

Confirm which bid strategy your active campaigns are using. If AI Max features matter to you and a campaign isn't on conversion-based bidding, that's the actual blocker, not a missing toggle.

Pull the new search term to landing page report for your worst-converting search terms. A mismatch between intent and landing page is a fixable problem; a genuinely bad keyword is a different one, and this report is how you tell them apart.

Don't plan around Performance Max device targeting being broadly available yet. It's still in pilot as of this November update, regardless of what an earlier October post implied about timing.

Frequently asked questions

What changed in Microsoft Advertising in November 2026?

Microsoft's November 2026 advertiser newsletter announced a campaign management interface overhaul already rolling out in accounts, AI Max becoming generally available across Microsoft Advertising accounts rather than a limited rollout, new search term to landing page reporting starting mid-November, and confirmation that Performance Max device targeting remains in pilot rather than open to all advertisers as an earlier October update had suggested.

Do I need to turn on AI Max to keep using Microsoft Advertising normally?

No, but if a campaign was already using predictive matching or autogenerated text assets, Microsoft has stated the corresponding AI Max setting gets enabled automatically for that campaign. Any AI Max feature requires conversion-based bidding, so a campaign running on a different bid strategy would need that changed first for AI Max features to apply.

Does Google Ads performance still matter if I also use Microsoft Advertising?

Yes. Microsoft Advertising reaches a meaningfully smaller, often different audience than Google Ads, and most small businesses running paid search still see Google Ads as the larger channel by volume. Microsoft Advertising updates like this one matter most if you are already running campaigns there or are considering adding it alongside Google Ads, not as a replacement for it.
