Google's AI Mode Cites Itself 4x More Than AI Overviews Do for Local Searches — What Two 2026 Studies Found

Two independent 2026 studies, looking at the same underlying shift from different angles, found a consistent pattern: Google's AI Mode favors its own Maps and Business Profile data for local-intent searches far more heavily than classic AI Overviews do — and that self-citation share has been climbing fast. Here's what Steady Demand's August citation audit and Profound's billion-plus-citation study actually measured, what's still unreplicated, and what it means for a local business's SEO priorities.

K
Kryvex Media Team
Nov 2, 2026 · 10 min read

"Optimize for AI search" has become a stock piece of SEO advice, but it treats Google's AI-generated answer surfaces as one thing when they're increasingly behaving like two very different systems with two very different citation habits — especially for local searches. Two separate 2026 studies, using different methodologies and measuring different things, both point the same direction on that split: Google's newer AI Mode is citing Google's own local data sources — Maps, Business Profile URLs — at a dramatically higher rate than classic AI Overviews do, and that self-citation tendency has been increasing over the course of the year. Here's what each study actually measured, because the two numbers getting cited around the web ("73.5%," "8.4x") come from different studies answering different questions, and conflating them overstates the certainty of either one.

Study one: Steady Demand's local-intent citation audit (August 2026)

Steady Demand, a local-SEO vendor, published a citation-source audit on August 22, 2026 covering 8,000 local-service searches and roughly 86,645 total citation references. Breaking local-intent queries out specifically, the study reported a sharp divergence by surface:

  • Classic AI Overviews, local-intent queries: about 73.5% of citations (out of 3,869 analyzed) went to the business's own website.
  • AI Mode, local-intent queries: about 79.8% of citations (out of a much larger 40,883 analyzed) went to Google Maps and Business Profile URLs — Google's own first-party local data — with only about 19.7% going to business websites.
  • AI Mode, informational (non-local-intent) queries: the pattern flips substantially — the business-site citation share rose to 47.5%, while Google's own self-citation share fell to 22.7%.

That third data point matters as much as the headline local-intent numbers: AI Mode isn't uniformly self-citing across every query type. The self-preference is concentrated specifically in local-intent search, where Google already owns the richest first-party dataset (Business Profile, Maps reviews, hours, photos) and has an obvious reason to serve that data directly rather than sending a user out to a third-party page that might be incomplete or out of date. The honest caveat here: this is a single-snapshot audit from one vendor, and one summary of the study notes that no other published research has independently replicated the AI Mode local-intent breakdown specifically. Treat the exact percentages as a real, directional signal rather than a settled industry-wide figure.

Study two: Profound's 1.44-billion-citation analysis (April–June 2026)

Profound, an AI-visibility analytics firm, ran a much larger-scale study from a different angle: analyzing roughly 1.44 billion AI Mode citations collected between April 15 and June 30, 2026. Its core finding, reported consistently across secondary coverage including Search Engine Journal and Gigazine, is that google.com's own citation share within AI Mode rose approximately 8.4x over that window — growth Profound attributes largely to Business Profile cards and Product Knowledge Panels specifically, rather than a broad-based increase across all citation types. One outlet reported that the growth makes google.com AI Mode's second most-cited domain overall. Profound also floated a possible connection to Google's broader "Universal Commerce Protocol" initiative, though that specific link is Profound's own interpretation and hasn't been confirmed by Google.

It's worth being precise about what this study does and doesn't show: citation share describes what AI Mode links to in its answers, not where click-through traffic actually ends up — a distinction Search Engine Journal's own coverage flags explicitly. A rising self-citation share is a real measurable shift in how AI Mode constructs its answers, but it is not, by itself, direct proof of how much referral traffic businesses are losing as a result. A separate, earlier SE Ranking study (cited for context, not as part of Profound's own numbers) found Google's self-citation share at 5.7% in June 2025 climbing to about 17.42% by early 2026 — a different methodology and time window from Profound's, so the two shouldn't be averaged together, but both describe the same underlying upward trend using independent data.

None of these studies are from Google itself, and their methodologies differ. What makes the pattern credible rather than a single outlier claim is that three separate research efforts (Steady Demand, Profound, and the earlier SE Ranking analysis), using three different approaches and time windows, all independently point toward the same direction: AI Mode's reliance on Google's own first-party local data, relative to independent business websites, has been increasing through 2026.

Why AI Mode behaves differently from classic AI Overviews here

The structural reason this split exists at all is that AI Mode and AI Overviews are genuinely different products built for different interaction patterns, not two names for the same feature. AI Overviews generate a short synthesized answer sitting above traditional search results, typically for a single query, and Google has historically positioned them as augmenting rather than replacing a results page a user can still scroll past. AI Mode is a more conversational, multi-turn search experience, and for a local-intent query — "best [service] near me," "[business type] open now," "is [business] good" — Google already has a highly structured, frequently updated, first-party dataset sitting right there in Maps and Business Profile. From a product standpoint, serving that structured data directly inside the AI Mode answer is a more defensible design choice for Google than for informational queries, where no comparable first-party dataset exists and sending users to independent sources remains the more useful answer — which is exactly the pattern Steady Demand's informational-query numbers show.

What this actually means for a local business's SEO priorities

The practical shift this supports isn't "stop investing in your own website" — informational-intent AI Mode answers still favored business websites at a much higher rate in the same study. It's a reallocation of where local-intent discovery effort should concentrate. A complete, accurate, actively maintained Google Business Profile — correct categories, complete hours, current photos, a steady stream of recent reviews, accurate service-area or address data — functions less like a secondary listing to "also keep updated" and more like a primary ranking asset specifically for the near-me, local-intent query class these studies measured. That's consistent with, not a departure from, what local SEO work has always emphasized; the citation-share data is a reason to treat Business Profile completeness as higher-priority and higher-stakes than it may have felt a year or two ago, not a reason to change strategy entirely.

It's also a reason to be skeptical of any claim — including some circulating already — that cites one of these studies' numbers ("73.5%" or "8.4x") as if it settles the whole question on its own. They measure different things, over different windows, with different methods, and the honest synthesis is "the direction is consistent across multiple independent sources, the exact magnitude is still being established."

Frequently asked questions

Does Google's AI Mode cite business websites less than AI Overviews for local searches?

According to an August 2026 citation audit by the local-SEO vendor Steady Demand, yes, for local-intent queries specifically. The study reported that classic AI Overviews sent roughly 73.5% of local-intent citations to business websites (out of 3,869 citations analyzed), while AI Mode sent roughly 79.8% of citations to Google Maps and Business Profile URLs and only about 19.7% to business websites (out of 40,883 citations analyzed). It is a single, unreplicated study, so treat the exact percentages as directional rather than definitive.

What did the Profound study find about Google citing itself in AI Mode?

Profound's analysis of roughly 1.44 billion AI Mode citations found that google.com's own citation share rose approximately 8.4x between April 15 and June 30, 2026, concentrated in Business Profile cards and Product Knowledge Panels — making Google itself AI Mode's second most-cited domain, according to secondary coverage of the study in Search Engine Journal and other outlets.

What should a local business actually do in response to this?

Treat a complete, accurate, actively maintained Google Business Profile as closer to a primary ranking asset than a secondary one, since both studies point the same direction: Google's own AI Mode in particular is favoring its own first-party local data sources over independent business websites. That doesn't make a business's own website irrelevant — informational queries in AI Mode still favored business sites at a much higher 47.5% rate in the same Steady Demand study — but it does mean Business Profile completeness and accuracy matter more for local-intent discovery specifically than they used to.
