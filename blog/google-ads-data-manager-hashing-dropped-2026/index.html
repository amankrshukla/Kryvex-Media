Google Ads Data Manager Drops Mandatory Hashing for Customer Data — What Changes November 16, 2026

Google Ads Data Manager is reported to stop requiring SHA-256 hashing of customer identifiers for Customer Match uploads starting November 16, 2026, per trade coverage from PPC Land and the German outlet drweb.de, both citing a Google advertiser notice. Here's exactly what's being reported, what's still genuinely unconfirmed, and what's actually worth checking in your own account rather than assuming anything has already changed.

K
Kryvex Media Team
Nov 8, 2026 · 9 min read

If you upload customer lists to Google Ads for Customer Match, a change is reportedly landing in your Data Manager settings on November 16, 2026 — and it touches a step every advertiser running first-party audience uploads has had to deal with for years: hashing. Per reporting from PPC Land and corroborating coverage from drweb.de, both citing a Google advertiser email reportedly sent around October 18, 2026, Google Ads Data Manager will stop requiring SHA-256 hashing of customer identifiers for Customer Match uploads, accepting encrypted customer data by default from that date instead. As of this writing, we could not find this change published on Google's own Ads Help Center or Data Manager API documentation, so this is reported as what trade press has found in an advertiser notice — not an independently confirmed Google announcement — and we'll flag exactly where the open questions are.

What Customer Match uploads have required until now

To understand why this change matters, it helps to remember what Customer Match has actually required advertisers to do. Customer Match lets a business upload its own first-party customer data — email addresses, phone numbers, mailing addresses — so Google Ads can match those customers against logged-in Google users and target or exclude them in campaigns. Because that data is sensitive, Google has required advertisers to hash each identifier using the SHA-256 algorithm before it ever leaves their system, so Google never receives a customer's raw email address or phone number, only an irreversible hashed version of it. Phone numbers additionally had to be formatted to the E.164 international standard (country code plus number, no spaces or symbols) before hashing. On top of the hashing requirement, Google has been migrating advertisers toward uploading Customer Match lists specifically through its Data Manager API — a move that, per earlier PPC Land reporting, Google made mandatory for Customer Match uploads by April 1, 2026, consolidating what used to be several separate upload paths into one.

What's reported to change on November 16

According to the trade coverage, starting November 16, 2026, Data Manager will accept encrypted customer data by default rather than requiring the SHA-256 hashing step advertisers have built their upload processes around. The reporting describes this as Google moving toward an encryption layer that functions differently from one-way hashing — notably, PPC Land's coverage references an April 2026 episode of Google's own Ads DevCast in which Google described this kind of encryption as optional and additional on top of "confidential matching," a privacy-preserving matching technique Google has been expanding across its ad products. That framing suggests the November 16 change is less about removing privacy protection and more about shifting which specific cryptographic method sits underneath it.

Here's the genuinely open question, and it's an important one for anyone with an existing, working upload pipeline: the reporting on this change does not say whether hashed uploads will still be accepted alongside the new default, or whether advertisers will eventually be required to switch upload methods entirely. Nor does it say which specific Data Manager API versions or upload paths are affected. Until Google's own documentation addresses this directly, the responsible assumption is that your current hashing-based process keeps working on November 16 and the new default is additive, not a forced migration — but that's an inference from the available reporting, not a guarantee.

A related, separate change worth knowing about

Trade coverage of the same advertiser notice also describes a second, distinct change: Google Ads is reported to begin ingesting hashed user data from all of an advertiser's connected sources simultaneously, reconciling the inputs internally rather than requiring the advertiser to select a single preferred data source. If accurate, that's a meaningfully different kind of change from the hashing update — it's about how Google combines signals it already receives from multiple integrations (a CRM connection, a website pixel-based list, an uploaded file, for instance) rather than about the cryptography applied to any one of them. The two changes are easy to conflate because they were apparently announced in the same notice and both concern Data Manager, but they affect different parts of the upload and matching pipeline.

Why this fits a broader 2026 pattern

Neither of these changes exists in isolation. Google has spent the better part of the past two years consolidating how advertisers connect first-party data to Google Ads, building Data Manager into the single required gateway for Customer Match and other first-party data uploads rather than leaving advertisers with several separate, inconsistent paths. That consolidation has tracked alongside the broader, industry-wide shift toward privacy-preserving and first-party-data-centric advertising as third-party cookies and legacy identifiers have become less reliable — confidential matching, enhanced conversions, and now, apparently, a revised approach to how uploaded identifiers get secured in transit are all pieces of the same underlying push. For a small or mid-sized advertiser, the practical implication isn't that any one of these individual changes is dramatic on its own — it's that the mechanics of how your customer data reaches Google Ads keep evolving, usually in the direction of more centralized tooling and more automatic privacy handling, which means an upload process that was set up correctly a year ago is worth periodically re-checking rather than assuming it's still the current best practice.

What to actually check in your own account

Given the genuinely unconfirmed parts of this story, here's what's worth doing rather than overreacting to a trade-press report:

Check your Google Ads account notifications in the days around November 16. If this change is real and rolling out as reported, Google typically notifies advertisers through in-product account notifications rather than requiring them to go looking for the change — the same pattern Google has used for other recent Data Manager and bidding updates.

Don't change a working hashing process preemptively. If your current Customer Match upload — whether it's a manual CSV export, a CRM integration, or an agency-managed pipeline — is correctly hashing identifiers with SHA-256 today, there is no indication from the available reporting that this process will stop working on November 16. Wait for your own account to show the change, or for Google's documentation to confirm it, before modifying anything.

Check your Data Manager API version if you have a developer-managed integration. If a developer or agency manages your Customer Match uploads programmatically, this is worth a direct conversation with them about which Data Manager API version your integration uses and whether they've seen anything about the hashing requirement changing for that specific version.

Treat this as a reminder to audit your broader first-party data setup, not just this one change. Whether or not the exact November 16 details play out as reported, the direction is clear enough: Google Ads is leaning further into Data Manager as the single pipe for first-party data. If your business still relies on older upload methods Google has been phasing out, this is a reasonable prompt to confirm you're on a supported path rather than one that works today but may not next year.

A note on why trade-press-only reporting deserves extra caution here

It's worth being explicit about why we're treating this particular story more cautiously than some other Google Ads changes we've covered. Updates like the August 2026 budget-limited bidding change or the Limited Ad Serving policy were reported with direct, attributable statements from a named Google spokesperson (Ads Liaison Ginny Marvin, in that case) relayed through established trade outlets like Search Engine Journal. This story is one step further removed: it traces back to an advertiser notice email that trade outlets reported on, rather than a public statement or an update to Google's own help documentation that we could check directly. That doesn't make it untrue — advertiser notices are a real and common way Google communicates account-level changes, and PPC Land has a solid track record of reporting on exactly this kind of Google Ads product notice accurately. But it does mean the specific mechanics (whether old hashing-based uploads keep working, which API versions are affected, whether this applies globally or in phases) are genuinely less certain than they would be with a direct Google statement, and we'd rather tell you that plainly than present secondhand reporting with false confidence.

Frequently asked questions

What is changing with Google Ads Data Manager on November 16, 2026?

Per trade press reporting from PPC Land and drweb.de, citing a Google advertiser notice, Google Ads Data Manager will stop requiring SHA-256 hashing of customer identifiers for Customer Match uploads starting November 16, 2026, accepting encrypted customer data by default instead.

Did Customer Match require hashing before this change?

Yes. Customer Match has required identifiers like email addresses and phone numbers to be hashed with SHA-256 before upload, with phone numbers also needing E.164 formatting, and uploads have been required to move through the Data Manager API since earlier in 2026.

Should I change how I upload Customer Match data right now?

Not yet. As of this writing, Google had not published the change on its own help center or developer documentation, and reporting leaves open whether hashed uploads will still be accepted. The safest step is to check your own Data Manager settings and Google Ads account notifications around November 16 before changing an existing, working upload process.
